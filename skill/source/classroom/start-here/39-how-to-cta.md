# How to CTA

_Start Here / 5. Promotion_

![image 1](images/start-here/39-how-to-cta-1.png)

Great, now we have some activity in the group and can use that as marketing material...

But how do we use community activity to CTA?

First, let's look at how NOT to CTA:

**These are the most common mistakes and how to fix them:**

**❌ **Just telling people to join your community without explaining why they should.

> "Join my community! It's only $50/mo!"

**✅ **Explain what's inside your group and connect it directly to the outcome they want.

> "Join to get your workouts reviewed every week so you can finally break through your plateau and start building muscle faster."

---

**❌ Creating your content first, then trying to force a CTA onto the end.**

> *Makes a video about meal prep...*  
> "Oh, and if you want, join my community to get courses and live calls!"

**✅ Decide on your CTA angle first, then build the content to naturally lead to it.**

> *Goal: Promote the weekly coaching calls in your skool*  
> Create content about top 10 common beginner mistakes in the gym, then end with:  
> "By the way, every Tuesday I review members' workouts in my community and help them fix mistakes like these. If you want to learn what's holding you back and start building muscle, join today."

---

**❌ Talking about your features, not the benefits.**

> "We have workout plans, weekly coaching calls, and an awesome community."

**✅** **Frame everything around what the member gets: the benefits.**

> "Join to get a personalized workout plan, weekly coaching to keep you accountable, and everything you need to finally lose the weight and build muscle."





Now that you know how to avoid beginner mistakes, in every CTA you should test 2 things

**Community Positioning: **Test calling out different things inside your community to see what people resonate with most (live calls, X resource in classroom, accountability, etc.). Once you know what works best, do more of those CTAs.

**Content: **Test different content styles that tee up your skool CTA differently. Here's 5 ideas:

- **Case Study Breakdown:** "How this member went from ___ to ___ (and the exact steps they followed)."
- **Reacting to Subscriber Mistakes:** "I reviewed 25 beginner submissions. Here are the 5 mistakes almost everyone made."
- **The 80/20 Guide:** "If you only had 30 days to learn ___, these are the only 5 things I'd focus on."
- **Myths vs. Reality:** "7 pieces of advice in ___ that sound smart... but actually slow you down."
- **Live Audit / Teardown:** "I analyzed a random subscriber's ___ and here's exactly what I'd change to get better results."

All of this reinforces your authority.

If someone watches one of these videos to the end and enjoys your advice, they'll likely appreciate the CTA because it gives them a way to keep learning from you.

> **Bottom line: **Your first CTAs probably won't be great - don't worry. This is an iterative process where you can only get better by DOING. **To get good fast, you should post 1 CTA per day **(the same exact way you got good at content creation).** **
