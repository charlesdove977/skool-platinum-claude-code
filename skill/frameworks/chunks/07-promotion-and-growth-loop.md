## Core Concept
Promotion for a Skool is a loop, not a one-way funnel: content brings people in, the community generates wins, questions, calls and friendships, and that activity becomes the next round of content. The creators earning $100k+ per month mostly do the same boring things at high volume: a single link pointing at the group, a CTA attached to a clear benefit, daily proof of members winning, and patience while small daily signup counts compound. Conversion rates barely differ between them (roughly 1.7% to 3.4% appear in the source); what separates them is how much qualified traffic they push to the about page, so the work is mostly "more eyeballs, pointed at one place, with a specific reason to click."

## Frameworks

### Framework: Slow-Compounding Math
**When to use:** When a launch brought 10 members instead of hundreds and it feels like a flop, or when judging whether a small daily signup number is worth continuing.
**Steps/Template:**
| Scenario | Numbers | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Launch "flop" | 10 members x $50/mo | $500 MRR |
| Steady drip | ~6 new members per day, 150 days (churn included) | roughly $35K MRR |
- The point of the arithmetic is that a modest, unglamorous daily number overshoots the program's milestones; the variable that matters is staying in the game long enough.
- A large follower count with few signups is treated as normal early on, not as evidence the idea is broken.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/37-big-idea.md. Mentor echo: Nick Saraev describes his own revenue as "slow and steady," a consistent adder of a little revenue at a time rather than an overnight spike (transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, billing/launch segment).

### Framework: Community Growth Loop
**When to use:** Any time you are deciding what to post next, or you feel you must invent content from nothing.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Promote the Skool with CTAs and test different ones to produce new signups.
2. New members plus the owner create activity inside: wins, live calls, networking, discussions, learnings.
3. That activity becomes marketing material.
4. Make content that features what is happening inside and shows people winning.
5. Use that content to test new CTAs, get more members, repeat.
- Key takeaway of the lesson: promote the community using what is happening in the community. The diagram's nodes are Social Media Content -> (Test Different CTAs) -> New Signups -> Skool (live calls, networking, member wins, community discussions, learning) -> Marketing Material -> back to content.
- Why it works: it creates fear of missing out. Watching members win repeatedly pulls spectators off the sideline.
- Unexpected events count as material too (the source mentions members getting married or getting Skool tattoos).
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/38-community-growth-loop.md (+ two flywheel images, IMAGE-NOTES-B.md). YM Babie (Trading Bootcamp) posts 3 to 5 member wins per day on her IG story, so a one-week follower has seen 20+ wins.

### Framework: Proof-as-Marketing-Material
**When to use:** When you have members with results (or even just social-comment praise) and need a repeatable daily post.
**Steps/Template:**
| Mentor | Proof format | Cadence / detail |
|---|---|---|
| YM Babie / Jade | Screenshots of member wins and reviews on Stories, link attached | Story every day, business-only account; reel plus caption CTA |
| Oscar Patel | One before-and-after transformation Story plus a reply, pushing to the Skool | One per day (5 to 6 per week at least); a two-week follower sees ~14 pieces of proof |
| Nick Saraev | Wins category pinned in the community; wins thread on the sidebar | Two of three pinned posts are wins (for conversion after the click) |
| Josh Madakor | Content built around helping a student inside the community (a support session recorded as a video) | Community support doubles as YouTube content |
- Jade's finding: the "inside the community" reels (live trading, big daily profits) get little reaction on Instagram, but she still posts them for credibility; her best traffic comes from trend-based lifestyle reels with the CTA in the caption.
- When you have no member proof yet, James Lu screenshotted TikTok comments and DMs praising him and posted them as the first item in his free group.

### Framework: CTA Mistakes and Fixes
**When to use:** Before writing any call to action in a video, post, caption or description.
**Steps/Template:**
| Mistake | Fix | Illustration in the lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Tell people to join without a reason | Name what is inside and tie it to the outcome they want | "Join to get your workouts reviewed every week so you can break through your plateau" beats "Join my community! It's only $50/mo!" |
| Make content first, bolt a CTA on the end | Pick the CTA angle first, then build content that leads naturally into it | Goal = promote Tuesday workout reviews, so the content is "top 10 beginner gym mistakes" ending in the Tuesday-review invitation |
| List features | Frame benefits the member gets | "Personalized workout plan, weekly coaching to keep you accountable" instead of "workout plans, weekly calls, a great community" |
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/39-how-to-cta.md. A member win reported that adapting a CTA-structure breakdown to an Instagram Reel produced 20+ new members in 2 days (classroom/start-here/03-3-past-wins.md, image 3).

### Framework: CTA Test Matrix and the 5 Content Formats
**When to use:** To run the daily CTA reps the course asks for, and to find which positioning pulls best.
**Steps/Template:**
Two variables to test in every CTA:
1. **Community positioning:** which part of the group you call out (live calls, a particular classroom resource, accountability, etc.). Keep doing the ones people respond to.
2. **Content style** that sets up the CTA. Five starters from the lesson:
| Format | Shape of the hook |
|---|---|
| Case-study breakdown | "How this member went from ___ to ___ and the exact steps" |
| Reacting to subscriber mistakes | "I reviewed 25 beginner submissions; here are the 5 mistakes almost everyone made" |
| 80/20 guide | "If you only had 30 days to learn ___, these are the only 5 things I'd focus on" |
| Myths vs reality | "7 pieces of advice in ___ that sound smart but slow you down" |
| Live audit / teardown | "I analyzed a random subscriber's ___; here's what I'd change" |
- These reinforce authority, so a viewer who finishes the video likely welcomes the invitation to keep learning.
- Cadence: post 1 CTA per day to get good fast, the same way content skill came from daily reps. First CTAs are expected to be weak.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/39-how-to-cta.md.

### Framework: Single-Link, Straight-to-Skool Rule
**When to use:** Setting up bios, channel links, descriptions, or when tempted to add a quiz, email list, lead magnet or second offer.
**Steps/Template:**
- Put exactly one link on Instagram and YouTube: the Skool. The lessons say even two links cuts traffic by roughly half.
- Remove competing CTAs: newsletters, Discord, Facebook groups, affiliate links, other offers.
- Send traffic directly to the about page rather than through a funnel layer. Nick reports a quiz funnel performs about the same or worse than sending straight to Skool, and saves steps; his own newsletter for Instagram also converted poorly.
- Josh Madakor avoided selling merch on YouTube because another link reduces clicks on the main link.
- Oscar Patel promotes nothing else: no 1:1 coaching, no brand offers, even after being pitched on Instagram "a thousand times."
- Nate Hook's tip relayed by Nick: the text beside the link matters. State the benefit, not just "join my community."
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/40-instagram-profile-checklist.md and 41-youtube-channel-checklist.md; transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md (ongoing-promotion section); 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md; 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md.

### Framework: Instagram Profile Checklist
**When to use:** Auditing or building an Instagram profile that must send people to the Skool.
**Steps/Template:**
| Check | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Link | The Skool is the only link in bio |
| Competing CTAs | Newsletter, Discord, Facebook group, affiliate links all removed |
| Daily Story | One Story a day driving to the community; a screenshot of a member post or win with a link counts, not always a hard sell |
| Pinned post | A pinned post promotes the community |
| Highlight | A Story Highlight collecting prior Skool stories |
| DMs | Everyone who DMs for help is replied to and pointed to the Skool |
| Comments | Comment askers are answered and pointed to the Skool |
| 10-second clarity | A visitor understands why to join within 10 seconds of landing (bio, pins, recent content share one promise) |
| Binge test | Someone watching your last 10 posts would naturally conclude joining is the next step (one narrative, not unrelated tips) |
| Visibility | The community is shown (community tab, discussions, live calls, member interactions, wins), not just mentioned |
| Bio line | Bio points to the link and states a benefit |
- Bio line contrast: "Join my Skool" is weak; "Get your personalized workout plan" (benefit) is the model.

### Framework: YouTube Channel Checklist
**When to use:** Auditing a channel's path to the Skool.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Skool is the only link on the channel (two links halves traffic).
2. Write a daily Community Post that drives to the community (a member win screenshot plus link is enough).
3. Bulk-update every older video description to a Skool CTA (YouTube's bulk-edit feature).
4. Make videos that attract the type of person who wants the Skool offer.
5. The community promotion should feel like the natural next step of the video, not randomly inserted.
6. Remove links to other offers.
7. Everywhere a written CTA lives (pinned comments, community posts, descriptions) explain what is inside and why to join, using a benefit line rather than "Join my Skool."
- Mentor texture: Nick's descriptions carry one or two links above the fold, which acts as a "soft pitch" without verbalizing an ad; he estimates about 90% of viewers scroll far enough to see them. James Lu's entire strategy is the first link in the description of evergreen videos.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/41-youtube-channel-checklist.md; 01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md.

### Framework: ManyChat Keyword Funnel (Specific, Not Generic)
**When to use:** Running "comment a word and get the link" on Instagram Reels.
**Steps/Template:**
The failure pattern: reel says "Comment GUIDE for more info" and the bot replies "Here's the info you requested." People do not know they are being sent to a paid community, so the click does not convert.
The fix, in order:
1. Identify the single most valuable thing inside the community (the one most likely to make someone join); if unsure, guess and test.
2. Make a Reel that leads naturally into that benefit.
3. At the CTA, be literal: point the camera at the laptop and show the resource, the live call on the calendar, or the challenge, and say it lives in your private community.
4. Add urgency in speech: "For the next 25 people who join, I'm [special offer]. Comment INVITE to claim your spot."
5. Automation step 1 (after they comment): confirm the scarcity and ask permission to send the link.
6. Automation step 2 (after they say yes): send the link and repeat the urgency and the outcome.
- Result: the follower already knows it is a community, what is inside, and that it is paid, so they are sold before landing on the about page.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/43-manychat.md (+ three screenshots).

### Framework: ManyChat Message Design (Jade's Model)
**When to use:** Writing the DM automation message itself, or deciding whether to add follow-ups.
**Steps/Template:**
- Build the first message from the questions people actually send you most often on Instagram; the message answers them up front.
- Put what comes with the community inside the DM, so the about page can stay minimal (Jade's has about three sentences and a generic image).
- One automation, opt-in, then the link. She tested one follow-up (offering an ebook if they did not click) and found it did nothing; she now sends no follow-ups because she dislikes being chased by automated messages.
- Her reels and Stories do not list modules or features; they sell the lifestyle and results, with the community as the CTA, because social viewers want instant gratification.
- TikTok has no ManyChat equivalent for her; on TikTok she tells viewers to click the bio link instead.
- Caution from James Lu: when he tried comment-keyword on TikTok without automation, he spent about eight hours a day messaging people manually and does not recommend it.
- Field update 2026-10-09: member answers: benefit-driven keyword and first message, urgency in a follow-up message; LeadConnector is an alternative that is cheaper at volume [community/questions/2026-10-09-manychat-messages.md; community/questions/2026-10-09-manychat-question.md].
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md (about page vs. ManyChat segment, then Stories segment); 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md (TikTok CTA question).

### Framework: TikTok Live Structure (the 3-part TLDR)
**When to use:** Planning a TikTok Live to produce many paid signups quickly.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Teach a specific lesson** for 1 to 2 hours of free value with no pitch.
2. **Ask who wants help** and step-by-step guidance.
3. **Offer the Skool** as the natural next step.
Reported yields: Samuel G averages 50+ signups per live (record 97 on one stream); Mikey Caloca 20+ per live; Jade started lives and had ~20 members from TikTok in the first two weeks, noting TikTok pushes lives out to new viewers all day while Instagram does not, and that TikTok live viewers ask more buying questions.
- Why it works: you reach strangers instantly and can build trust in real time; Samuel says a stranger needs roughly one to two hours of education before paying even $9 to $25.
- In Mikey Caloca's short example, the host asks the chat to guess the monthly price (guesses of $50 to $500), then reveals $8, mentions a refund promise, class cadence, member count (about 2.2K), in-person meetups, and sends people to the bio link.
- Field update 2026-10-09: member guide pinned by staff: 3 to 4 topics with a CTA after each, demonstration marketing, drive comments and watch time, make the CTA obvious on screen but never show or say the link (the algorithm restricts it) [community/discussion/2026-10-08-using-tiktok-live-to-grow-your-skool-group.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/42-tiktok-live.md; transcripts/start-here/42-tiktok-live.md (Mikey's guess-the-price reveal, opening seconds [0:00:00] block); 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md.

### Framework: Anatomy of a 97-Signup Live (Samuel G's full session)
**When to use:** Building a long-form live that earns the pitch. The recording is 1:55:29; the transcript carries a single timestamp, so positions below are approximate.
**Steps/Template:**
| Phase | What Samuel does | Approx. position |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Curiosity hook | Opens mid-conversation with a surprising claim (houses at ~3.5% interest with no credit and no down payment) and promises to prove it live | first minutes [0:00:00] |
| 2. Live proof | Searches public listings on screen with a repeatable list of filter steps; shows real properties; asks the chat to pick cities so it feels custom; repeats "the steps are identical in every city" | first ~20 to 40 minutes |
| 3. Poll the chat constantly | "Yes or no?", "put a 1 in the chat", "who joined late?" every minute or two to feed engagement signals | throughout |
| 4. Break limiting beliefs one by one | Names the objection (not a veteran, no credit, what if the bank finds out), answers it with a document or source, then asks the chat if the belief is "disqualified" | middle |
| 5. Diagram and story | Draws a seller/buyer diagram with named characters so a first grader can follow; explains the mechanism simply | middle |
| 6. Personal story | Being rejected by banks, paying $12,500 for a seminar and $30,000 more for hand-holding, then getting results; ties it to the mission of helping ordinary people | roughly 55 to 67% through |
| 7. Qualify | "Who actually wants help, not just needs it?" and picks a small number of people (15) instead of selling to the whole room | roughly 67 to 72% through |
| 8. Price anchoring | Names the market price ($15,000, then $1,000 as "fair"), then rejects those, lands on a normal price ($47), then a live-only deal ($25) for the first 15 | roughly 70 to 76% through |
| 9. Scarcity and speed | Link stays up 30 seconds; counts down spots left ("13 left... 5 left... last one"); says to come back and confirm you joined | roughly 76 to 84% through |
| 10. Upsell | Offers an annual option ($200 for the year, prorated upgrade of $175 for those who already paid $25); walks viewers through the upgrade clicks | roughly 87 to 95% through |
| 11. Answer logistics | Where the link lives (a Service Plus form button), what the calendar looks like (five live meetings a week), time zone, "no gatekeeping" | late |
- His stated reason for charging anything: free materials get ignored; a small payment creates accountability. He also says he handed out contracts and scripts free for almost a year and nobody acted.
- Caveat on this session: it is one niche's rhythm (real-estate education). Samuel himself says the framework is niche-specific and must be rebuilt for each topic.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/07-samuel-g-tiktok-live-example.md (offer/price section, approximately 1h15m to 1h50m by position in the 1:55:29 recording); 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md (framework explanation).

### Framework: Live Retention and Energy Rules
**When to use:** Preparing any live; reviewing a recording that did not grow.
**Steps/Template:**
- **Start immediately.** The opening mistake is staring at the camera waiting for five more people. Start teaching at once; the algorithm learns who you are from what you say and then delivers the right viewers (Samuel).
- **Plain words.** If a first grader cannot follow it, simplify. He rebuilt his language after 45 days of lives where jargon (novation, wraparound mortgage) lost the audience.
- **Do not sell early.** Selling only to those who stay an hour or more filters out people without attention span.
- **Address doubts and objections** and show visual proof.
- **Stand up** during the live to lift energy, voice and body language; Samuel notes the platform appears to read tone, volume and body language, not just words.
- **Have fun.** His Mario video game on a background TV exists because viewers kept calling him Mario; he leaned in.
- **Haters:** one comment is not the audience; respond lightly, or let a moderator mute disruptive viewers, and keep going.
- **Questions:** answer the ones that fit the flow; one viewer's question is not everyone's.
- **Do not wait to be good.** The first 3 to 5 lives will be rough; he went live for 45 days straight (even at lunch) with 10 to 15 viewers before it clicked, then built a framework from it.
- **Do not script.** Go live, talk, and let viewers' questions reveal the message, then organize it after.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/42-tiktok-live.md ("how to keep viewers watching" bullets); 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md (best practices and first steps).

### Framework: Live Affiliate Flywheel
**When to use:** When a proven live format exists and you want it multiplied without you hosting every session.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Create the framework (what to say at the start, middle, end, and when to invite).
2. Train a regular person to run it. Samuel's first trainee was an in-house transaction coordinator in his 60s who had never used TikTok; he reached 10 to 20 signups per live, then 150K followers in four months, going live two to three times a day.
3. Review their lives (a team plus AI scoring), give feedback twice a week and one-on-one, and track improvement from 10 to 20 viewers to hundreds.
4. Pay members who refer: Samuel's community pays 20% to any member (pro affiliates or ordinary members). About 80% of his traffic was reported to come from affiliates at the time of the interview.
5. Use a separate affiliates-only Skool for training.
- Josh Madakor set Skool's built-in affiliate at 10%, and was considering raising it to 30%, comparing it to Growth Boost since someone else gets customers for you.
- Nick Saraev's affiliate offer in the welcome flow "isn't working super well" for him.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md (affiliate passage and affiliate-percentage question); 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md (affiliates question).

### Framework: Growth Boost Optimization
**When to use:** After launching, to turn on Skool's own distribution; before touching the cover image.
**Steps/Template:**
- **What it is:** (1) Skool runs Meta and Instagram ads straight to your group; (2) Skool promotes your group in Skool search and on the homepage. Onboarding materials describe a 30M+ (31M in the call) user base and a typical ~30% signup bump from discovery.
- **Step 1, enable and bid:** Settings -> Discovery, Growth Boost on (default). Default bid 30%; 70% is the most popular among those who change it. Higher bid = more members at lower margin. No Growth Boost fee on customers you bring yourself (the call notes only the standard ~2.9% transaction fee then).
- **Step 2, cover image:** upload an image following the cover-image best practices. If you see no Skool Network visitors or signups after a week, test a meaningfully different version (new headline or promise), not a color tweak. The cover image is the very first ad run on every group.
- **Step 3, traffic, conversion, retention:** send traffic to an about page that converts and retains; Skool favors groups that convert and keep members month over month.
- **Ad creative upgrade:** once the cover ad works, Skool may use your organic content as ads. A member win credits ~$10K MRR in a week to Growth Boost and guesses two causes: the ad was clipped from content that had already gone viral, and the community was active enough (she showed up daily) to keep free-trial members.
- Josh Madakor: after he and a colleague listed every Growth Boost recommendation in a spreadsheet and had Claude generate a fix list, traffic arrived; Growth Boost and Skool Network now sit beside YouTube as his biggest sources, and he calls Growth Boost essentially zero-risk advertising.
- An example Growth Boost analytics card shows 5,208 visitors, 92 signups (1.8%), with roughly 20% of visitors from the Skool network.
- Field update 2026-10-09: Hormozi's 10 tips: connect socials so Skool can run ads; warm the group with your organic audience first (Skool will not push an empty group); about 40% annual discount; start with the revshare slider HIGH and dial back once it works; face on the cover; outcome promise; VSL + offer + proof (NOMAD); CTAs about the promise; more CTAs, repeat winners; go all in [community/updates/2026-10-09-tips-to-get-more-traffic-from-skool-ads.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/44-bonus-growth-boost.md; transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/08-onboarding-call.md (Growth Boost segment); 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md (growth boost passage); classroom/start-here/03-3-past-wins.md (images 2 and 5).

### Framework: CTA Intensity Dial
**When to use:** Deciding how hard to sell inside content, given your audience and platform.
**Steps/Template:**
| Setting | Who | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Nearly silent | James Lu | No spoken CTAs; link first in description; about three evergreen videos did the work |
| Whisper (99:1) | Nick Saraev | Mostly value, a tiny soft pitch; his first explicit pitch at the end of a very long video drew a huge response; he lets Skool descriptions and pinned comment do the advertising |
| Moderate | Josh Madakor | A CTA near the end of educational videos, "not too invasive" |
| Constant | Oscar Patel, Jade | Daily Story CTA, caption CTAs, and an intro that pitches the community; Oscar says repetition is tolerated when the value is high; Jade says commercials work through familiarity |
- Oscar's one-week sprint answer: double content frequency at the same quality, then raise CTA frequency and announce something with a date.
- Jade posts only when trying to sell and accepts losing followers if revenue rises.

### Framework: Cold-Start Hype Sequence
**When to use:** First weeks of a launch when the audience hasn't heard of the Skool.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Mention the community at the end of every video or reel and use the same phrase repeatedly so people associate it with you (Oscar repeated "health equals looks").
2. Post a daily Story with a countdown or hype message.
3. Aim for people to follow and see it, not for a single viral post.
4. Result for Oscar: ~80 members at $39/month from TikTok before he had IG or YouTube content, then he scaled by adding platforms.
- Jade moved the members of her paid private Instagram page (about 10,000 in year one) onto Skool and had 1,500 members in an hour; those who did not join immediately came later because she stayed consistent.
- Nick soft-launched by adding a join line to the top of his YouTube descriptions late at night and woke to a few hundred dollars of MRR; he deliberately launches uncertain things at night so he is not tempted to check numbers.
- Samuel G launched to past free-Zoom attendees: ~150 attended the "last Zoom", about 70 joined that night.

### Framework: Member-Count Price Squeeze
**When to use:** Creating urgency without a fixed countdown.
**Steps/Template:**
- Announce that the price rises when membership hits a number (for example 1,100), not on a date.
- Nick observes the signup rate speeds up as the count approaches the threshold, unlike date-based deadlines.
- Jade says raising prices did not slow her traffic (people kept buying); Josh Madakor went the other way and cut his price sharply after finding each member costs him only about $5 a month in infrastructure (the transcript's exact figures are garbled).
- The ManyChat lesson's "next 25 people" offer uses the same logic in a CTA.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md (price increase question); 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md; classroom/start-here/43-manychat.md.

## Key Principles
- Show the community in the content. Screens of the community tab, calls and wins beat any description of the features.
- Daily is the unit. One CTA, one Story, one Community Post per day; improvement comes from reps.
- Decide the CTA before the content. A content piece without a destined CTA tends to end with a tacked-on plug.
- Benefits outrank features, both in bio lines ("Get your personalized workout plan") and in CTAs.
- One link, one promise. Every extra link or offer dilutes clicks and attention.
- Pre-sell outside the page. Several mentors say the about page matters less than what the viewer experienced before clicking.
- A specific DM beats a generic one. Tell people they are joining a paid community and what is inside before the link appears.
- Teach first on live. Value for an hour or more filters for buyers; the pitch follows the proof.
- Consistency over cleverness. Nick's central claim: showing up daily for a long period beats strategy.
- Hedge platforms but keep one lead. Josh keeps staff for secondary platforms in case the lead channel is lost.
- Let Skool amplify what already works. Growth Boost favors groups that convert and retain, so improving the member experience is part of promotion.

## Decision Tools
- IF launch results are only a handful of members THEN use the daily-signup math (6 per day over 150 days) to judge direction instead of calling it a flop; the lesson treats this as normal.
- IF you have a clear CTA angle (e.g., weekly reviews) THEN build content that teaches the problem the angle solves, then end with the invitation.
- IF your CTA says "Join my community" THEN add the outcome and one concrete thing inside before posting.
- IF your bio has two or more links THEN remove everything but the Skool; the course cites roughly 50% traffic loss from a second link.
- IF a ManyChat funnel gets comments but few conversions THEN rewrite the on-camera CTA to show the resource and the paid-community framing, and add a scarcity line plus a two-step DM.
- IF you can produce an hours-long educational stream or video on a topic with broad curiosity THEN use a live to earn trust before pitching; ask who wants help, then offer the Skool.
- IF you have no member wins yet THEN use social comments and DMs praising your help as the first proof assets.
- IF you sell on Instagram and your members are impulse-driven THEN sell lifestyle/outcome in the content and put the details in the DM or description.
- IF you sell a tool-learning topic requiring long attention THEN lean toward YouTube for conversion (Nick's 20x data point).
- IF no Skool Network traffic or signups after a week of Growth Boost THEN test a materially different cover headline or promise.
- IF your community is good but traffic is flat THEN raise content volume first (Oscar: at least 2x at same quality), then CTA frequency.
- IF you have affiliate-capable members THEN set a meaningful referral rate (the interviews show 10% considered raised to 30%, and 20% in use).
- IF you are afraid people will tire of CTAs THEN remember the Oscar and Jade position: if the value is high enough, repetition is accepted, and a non-follower is not your buyer anyway.

## Anti-Patterns
- Generic ManyChat keyword ("GUIDE") with a bland reply: people click without knowing it costs money, and bounce.
- Making a video, then appending "join my community" with no tie to the content.
- Listing features (plans, calls, community) instead of what the member gets.
- Multiple links, newsletter signups, Discord or merch links beside the Skool: each competes for the click.
- Waiting for more viewers to join a live before starting; the stall kills retention.
- Selling in the first minutes of a live, or using jargon the audience does not know.
- Sitting down for a long live; energy drops and the audience feels it.
- Judging growth by follower counts or conversion percentage rather than about-page traffic and signups.
- Changing the cover, CTA, content style and page all at once: nothing can be traced to its cause.
- Following up on automated DMs repeatedly (Jade's follow-up produced nothing and annoys recipients).
- Piling on funnel steps (quiz, landing page, newsletter) when the mentor data shows direct-to-Skool performs as well or better.
- Putting fear of the first weak CTAs ahead of the daily-rep habit the course recommends.
