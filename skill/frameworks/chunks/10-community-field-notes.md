## Core Concept
The Platinum feed in its first two weeks is a snapshot of what newly accepted owners actually struggle with. Most arrive with an existing audience (often 100k to 1M+ followers) and a half-built group, and nearly every real question reduces to a handful of things: how much to charge and whether to start free, how to get more traffic to the about page, how to keep people from quietly cancelling, how to sell a higher tier, how to run live video, and how Platinum's own rules work. With the comments now captured, the feed does carry real answers, and almost all of the authoritative ones come from a single person, Mark N. of the Skool team, who replied to roughly forty threads. His consistent line: charge from day one at a low founding price, never start with a free tier, send all traffic to the about page, keep the offer to one clear sentence, treat the live call plus daily presence as the retention engine, and launch before anything is polished. Members add useful tactics (TikTok Live scripts, OBS multistreaming, downgrade offers, manual one-to-one invites) but also repeat some wrong or unverified beliefs, so this chunk labels every answer as STAFF, MEMBER, MENTOR or Inference. Use it to recognise which common question you are really asking and to get the best-sourced answer to it.

#### Q4. Why TikTok Live over Instagram Live, and how do I convert on a live?
**Asked in:** why-tiktok-live-but-not-instagram-live (2026-10-04); how-to-increase-conversions-from-tiktok-live (2026-10-06); tiktok-live win (2026-10-03); drive-more-traffic (2026-10-07).
**What the posters said:** Member 83f3 streamed the same pastry live on both platforms: Instagram 102 total viewers (52 peak), TikTok 5,300+ unique viewers (218 peak, 89 average), with only 2 sales at EUR 29/month despite CTAs every 15 to 20 minutes. Member 37f1 reported 31 members from 12 daily hour-long lives.
**Answers from comments:**
- **MEMBER, Member 37f1 (the win poster):** on a live she teaches fully and answers questions, because the paid product is coaching during implementation; she mentions the community naturally; a chatbot gave her talking points until she memorised them; the room starts at 1 to 3 people, so talk to that one person; daily consistency trains the algorithm; day 13 hit 108 people at once; she lives Mon to Fri 9 to 10 am ET for 60 to 90 minutes; webinars for four months to sell a course had been exhausting [wins/2026-10-03-tiktok-live.md, 2026-10-03]. In another thread she noted she converts better when she mentions the CTA throughout the live, and waiting for one big end pitch is why some days did not convert [questions/2026-10-04-why-tiktok-live-but-not-instagram-live.md].
- **MEMBER, Member 98b3:** use the first lives to build trust; then mention the offer roughly every 15 to 20 minutes; aim for about 80% value and 20% selling.
- **MEMBER, Member 5291:** CTAs about every 10 minutes in varied wording (join the program, module 2 goes deeper, office hours); verified business accounts can use TikTok's in-app "Services+" request flow so viewers stay in the live and you capture their contact for follow-up; post your popular topics just before going live to pull organic viewers. She also says trust is quicker on TikTok but suggests testing both for a few weeks because some audiences behave differently.
- **MEMBER, Member cd2f:** a three-step pull: ask viewers to type a number if they have the problem, show a case study or false-belief story, then ask them to type a word if they want that result (a trial close).
- **MEMBER, Member d614 and Member cd2f:** TikTok shows lives to new people constantly, so it is a numbers game; show up Mon to Fri for an hour; free traffic to test content and CTAs before paying for ads [questions/2026-10-06-how-to-increase-conversions-from-tiktok-live.md; questions/2026-10-07-drive-more-traffic.md].
- **MEMBER Instagram Live evidence (negative):** Member a3b5: 256 attendees, 129 comments, 5 keyword triggers, 0 signups in a 30-minute IG live. Member 83f3: a two-hour IG pastry workshop with a 7-day trial drew 390+ comments, 150 people, 72 at peak and 0 subscriptions. Member 37f1: views on IG Live but no conversions.
- **STAFF, Mark N.:** pointed Member 37f1 to the classroom lesson on TikTok Live; no structure rules were given in comments.
- **MENTOR (transcripts):** TikTok pushes lives to new viewers all day, Instagram does not; Samuel G teaches plain-language value first with a short link window [03, ~0:33:05 to 0:34:18; 06, ~0:13:01; 07, ~1:16:53].
- Net answer: comments mostly reject the earlier inference of "one pitch at the end only". The practical consensus is repeated, varied CTAs every 10 to 20 minutes inside a value-led live, a clear way to act (link in bio, Services+ request, or keyword), and daily consistency. Member 83f3's reach was not the problem; his conversion and 41-second average watch time were.

## Key Principles
- Weigh answers by source: STAFF (Mark N., Claire Q.) first, then mentor transcripts, then peers. Treat peer-quoted mentor advice as unconfirmed.
- Launch the day you are accepted; polish later. The most-liked non-staff win post of the week (30 likes) was a one-line "launched today".
- Charge from day one at a founding price below your instinct; avoid a free tier; a free trial is the staff-endorsed barrier-reducer.
- Traffic is the dominant constraint: at the staff benchmark of about 1.3% for $100k/mo groups (and 1 to 5% in member data), hundreds or thousands of weekly about-page visitors are needed.
- Send traffic to the about page, post a daily story, run 3 to 4 reels a week with CTAs, and put a CTA on every long video.
- A live event is the highest-yield place to ask for money: both the upsell win and the TikTok Live win came from live formats, and live CTAs work best when repeated and varied.
- The first win and ongoing live support drive retention; a short chronological classroom, a weekly live call and daily presence beat more content.
- Reward the behaviour you want in the community (raffle for posting deals; staff approved).
- Differentiate paid tiers by access and support, not by hiding information; sell high-ticket as limited seats.
- Make sure every revenue stream you track counts as recurring if you want it to count in Platinum rankings, and focus on one group.
- Ask specific questions: name the metric, the number and the choice you are torn between; specific asks got the sharpest staff replies.
- Use AI prompt documents for fulfilment steps that tutorials fail to teach.

## Decision Tools
- IF a member asks "free first or paid?" → THEN follow staff: charge from day one at a founding price; if you want a lower barrier, use a free trial, not a free tier.
- IF an existing free group is large → THEN convert it to a low-ticket paid tier, grandfather current free members, and point them at the plans page to upgrade.
- IF an early-bird price is planned → THEN use a window of one to two weeks or a member cap (first 25), not an open-ended sale.
- IF a question is about a feature the feed does not answer (webinars for non-members, multi-group routing, Kit data sync) → THEN ask the Skool team (Mark N. or Claire Q.) rather than guess; webinars for non-members were not confirmed by staff.
- IF conversion is at or above 1% to 3% with modest traffic → THEN the issue is traffic volume, not the page (staff benchmark 1.3% for $100k/mo groups).
- IF conversion is below 1% with solid traffic → THEN check offer clarity and price friction before redesigning the page.
- IF a live produces reach without sales → THEN add a CTA every 10 to 20 minutes in varied wording, use a way to capture contact, and keep going daily.
- IF an Instagram Live gets comments but no signups → THEN test TikTok Live for a few weeks before judging either platform.
- IF a member says they love the community but cancel for time → THEN offer a downgrade tier, annual plan or pause, and rebuild the first win.
- IF churn is flat despite a good checklist → THEN look at who stays after month 1 and 2 and move that behaviour earlier in onboarding.
- IF a member never opened the classroom → THEN make a human first-step call instead of a form email.
- IF the classroom feels heavy → THEN cut to a short chronological path with action-item breaks.
- IF you want to upsell → THEN run a limited-seat invitation inside a live member session, and use a waitlist with manual invites where you can.
- IF a request asks "review my page" → THEN ask what single metric they want to improve before reviewing.
- IF revenue is one-time paid → THEN convert it to recurring or accept that it does not count toward rankings.
- IF you run more than one group → THEN pick one for Platinum and still keep Growth Boost on for the others.
- IF paid ads cost more than the first-month price → THEN check whether month-two profit still holds before cutting them.
- IF you feel pulled to a second topic → THEN postpone it; the cohort rule is no distractions.
- IF you are stuck on a name → THEN choose the clearer one (staff: clear beats clever) and rely on the one-sentence offer.

## Anti-Patterns
- Posting a link and asking "thoughts?" with no metric or question.
- Treating member replies as official: several peers recall mentor advice inaccurately; check staff or the course.
- Starting with a free tier or relying on a free group as the engine.
- Selling the product as a six-week program or one-time course inside a membership business.
- Waiting for a perfect name, video or curriculum before launch.
- Splitting traffic or revenue across several groups or tiers when one destination would do.
- Moving premium members to WhatsApp or another off-platform chat.
- Pitching only once at the end of a live, or never mentioning the offer at all.
- Overloading the classroom with extra modules.
- Letting engaged-looking cancellations ("I love it, no time") go unaddressed.
- Offering giveaways or incentives without checking platform terms (staff approved the airpods raffle; still check yours).
- Assuming one-time revenue counts toward the Platinum leaderboard.
- Quoting peer-reported churn numbers as benchmarks; no staff benchmark was captured for churn.
