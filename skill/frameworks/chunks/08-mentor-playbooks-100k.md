## Core Concept
Six owners making roughly $100k to $250k a month run six different businesses, in niches as far apart as AI agencies, looks and health, day trading, cybersecurity, SAT prep and no-bank real estate, yet they converge on a small set of habits: one narrow promise, a classroom that is deliberately thin at launch, the owner personally present every day, proof stacked everywhere, and traffic pointed straight at the Skool page rather than through funnels. They disagree on the things that are genuinely taste or niche dependent (price level, free trials, tiers, CTA frequency, platform, team size), and the disagreement is useful because it shows which levers are optional. Use this chunk as a menu of proven operating models: pick the mentor whose audience, content style and capacity most resemble yours, copy their sequence and numbers as a starting hypothesis, then test in the market rather than in your head.

### Framework: Nick Saraev Operating Model (Maker School)
**When to use:** You sell a skill-to-income outcome (agency, freelancing, a trade), you are a solo operator, and you can publish long educational video. Also the best model for how to validate an offer with almost nothing built.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Launch with the smallest possible product, then ask people to criticize it.**
   - First product was a 14-day agency program plus one monthly call on the calendar [~0:02:17, cue 49]. He DM'd 20 to 30 people from his Twitter replies, got their feedback (mostly negative), compiled the complaints and rebuilt the curriculum [~0:03:24, cue 73]. Core belief: test in the market, not in your mind [~0:01:43, cue 37].
   - He had roughly 5k to 10k followers at the time [~0:02:51, cue 61].
2. **Soft launch at night.** He edited his YouTube links to say "join my community" just before bed so a flop would not hurt, and woke to a few hundred dollars of MRR [~0:04:32, cue 97].
3. **Price low, then raise.** Started around $46-47/mo (no formula; he looked at communities charging $20 to $100) and reasoned that raising is easier than cutting [~0:05:05 to 0:06:13, cues 109-133]. Now about $184/mo. His stated regret: he raised too far, too fast, which priced out part of his audience and cost network effect; he would trade some revenue per member for more members [~0:20:14 to 0:20:48, cues 433-445]. Trigger price increases by member count ("price rises at N members") rather than by date, because join rate speeds up as the threshold nears [~0:52:12, cue 1117].
4. **One boolean promise with a guarantee.** "Land your first AI client in 90 days or full refund." He chose 90 days by testing 30 days (no), a year (yes), six months, then three months [~0:09:34 to 0:10:42, cues 205-229]. Refunds run about 1 to 3% (roughly 10 requests the prior month); he estimates the guarantee roughly tripled signups, an upside of about 300% against a 1 to 3% cost [~0:11:49 to 0:12:56, cues 253-277]. Hosts name this a "boolean offer": it is either true or false (a client landed or not) rather than a fuzzy improvement.
5. **About page = proof wall.** First video was a dull, half-hour Google-doc walkthrough that still converted; a polished VSL later was no better, so he plans a casual rerecord [~0:14:37 to 0:16:18, cues 313-349]. The page carries hundreds of win screenshots, the offer high on screen, a keyword (AI automation) in title and copy, an ROI frame ($100 into the market vs $100 into Maker School), "what's inside is worth $40,000", daily Q&A with him as a bonus and a price-increase scarcity line [~0:16:52 to 0:20:14, cues 361-433]. Uploading the about video through Loom (so it animates) lifted conversion [~0:57:15, cue 1225].
6. **Promotion: 99% value, 1% pitch.** One hard pitch at the end of an hour-long video, plus a benefit line and link at the top of every description and a pinned comment [~0:22:29 to 0:24:43, cues 481-529; ~0:49:24, cue 1057]. Promote straight to Skool, no quiz funnel, newsletter or landing page [~0:49:58, cue 1069]. YouTube converts about 20x Instagram, which also matches a 20x watch-time gap [~0:48:17, cue 1033]. Skool discovery is 28.1% of his traffic against a platform average he cites as about 30% [~0:58:22, cue 1255].
7. **Content style:** long, unscripted, low-production talking-head tutorials (including multi-hour guides) because solo creators cannot sustain heavy editing [~0:26:24 to 0:30:53, cues 565-661]. The hosts add that several of the top earners they studied publish multi-hour free guides [~0:29:12, cue 625].
8. **Daily owner routine (about 1 hour):** every morning reply to or like 75 to 100% of posts from the last 24 hours; ship one small improvement to the community each day (example: adding "AI automation" to the community title moved him from page 2 to page 1 of Skool search) [~0:31:27 to 0:34:15, cues 673-733]. Use a speech-to-text tool because speaking is 150 to 200 wpm vs 30 to 50 typed [~0:44:21 to 0:45:28, cues 957-973]. Keep the activity chart green; a missed day is rare (five or six in a year) [~0:47:09, cue 1009].
9. **Classroom stays simple:** a resource library (templates and skills), weekly one-hour call recordings, "community exclusives" (20-minute videos answering repeated questions, cut his support time from 70 to 80 min/day to 15 to 40), a couple of tool courses, and a day-by-day main program for 31 days with no days off [~0:36:30 to 0:41:33, cues 781-889]. Start Here is a short numbered sequence; competitors' classrooms were so cluttered members absorbed under 5% of the content [~0:42:40 to 0:44:21, cues 913-949].
10. **Retention and goodwill:** treats the group as a "third place" people visit daily; members start answering each other [~0:46:02 to 0:47:09, cues 985-1009]. If someone finishes 90 days without a client but wants to stay, he sometimes refunds a month so the win later feels bigger [~0:56:42, cue 1213].
11. **Capacity rule:** constrain community time (he runs an agency alongside it) because unlimited time spent in the group does not scale value [~0:55:34 to 0:56:08, cues 1189-1201]. Spend about 10 minutes a day helping in the Skool owners community; a long post there brought many members [~0:58:56, cue 1261].

### Framework: Oscar Patel Operating Model (Health = Looks)
**When to use:** You have a visual transformation or strong personal-origin story, a low-priced mass-market offer, and you can post proof daily. Best for volume-first traffic strategies.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Plan the community from day one.** He wrote a 27-step plan the day before his first TikTok; step 5 was launching the Skool, step 14 was 100 members. Built a community, not a course, from the start [~0:03:06 to 0:03:37, cues 73-85].
2. **One mission line repeated at the end of every video.** "Health is looks" (a single sentence anyone can repeat) [~0:22:01, cue 517; ~0:55:16, cue 1297]. Test: if you cannot say what you do in one line, tighten it.
3. **Niche discipline.** Bring every topic back into your niche instead of expanding sideways (he reframes foot or gait issues as "the way you walk shapes your face"). When he drifted into diet a few months in, it failed [~0:30:43 to 0:32:15, cues 721-757].
4. **Cold-start launch:** with about 40k TikTok followers and about 1k Instagram followers he hyped it with daily stories, countdowns and a mention at the end of every reel, and opened with 80 members at $39/mo [~0:22:01 to 0:23:03, cues 517-541].
5. **Traffic is a numbers game:** conversion held between about 3% at peak and about 2.1% now, so he optimizes for more visitors, not for tweaking the funnel; no free guides, free trials or test periods [~0:12:18 to 0:13:20, cues 289-313]. The lesson page cites about 70k about-page visitors a month.
6. **Daily proof cadence:** one before-and-after story per day (five or six a week) with a reply link to the community, a YouTube mention in every video, intro-with-credentials plus a harder CTA after value [~0:13:51 to 0:16:24, cues 325-385]. One transformation reel reached roughly 45M views [~0:20:29, cue 481]; recent conversion mostly comes from speaking videos.
7. **Sell through overwhelm, not scarcity of information.** Give away the framework; the paid product is time saved, a routine and accountability for $39 [~0:16:24 to 0:18:27, cues 385-433].
8. **Seven-day sprint plan (his answer to the Platinum cutoff):** at least double content volume at equal quality, up to one high-quality YouTube video a day if possible, raise CTA frequency, and announce or launch something inside the group to create a reason to join [~0:37:22 to 0:38:54, cues 877-913].
9. **Access to the owner is the product, scaled with coaches.** Once past about 3,000 members he added coaches, wrote a welcome message pointing to them, kept a live call every Sunday and still answers urgent DMs (about 15 a day, 20 minutes) [~0:39:55 to 0:41:58, cues 937-985]. Hires come only from his own community and family [~0:53:44, cue 1261].
10. **Retention:** phases and levels were added early to keep churn down; release the most important routine first and unlock later phases after a wait, but never add locked phases after launch or change price repeatedly [~0:50:40 to 0:51:10, cues 1189-1201; ~0:57:19, cue 1345].
11. **One change at a time.** Change the VSL, about page, structure and CTAs sequentially and give each about a week of data [~0:33:16 to 0:35:19, cues 783-829]. New ideas are tested on himself for about two months before entering the paid classroom [~0:56:17, cue 1321].
12. **Reject distractions.** He refuses brand deals, one-to-one coaching promotion and other offers so the audience sees exactly one call to action [~0:43:30 to 0:44:31, cues 1021-1045].

### Framework: Jade Operating Model (Trading Bootcamp)
**When to use:** You have a personality-led Instagram or TikTok audience, a lifestyle-linked outcome, and want a nearly zero-friction funnel (ManyChat to Skool). Strong model for converting followers into paying members without teaching content.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Start with access to you plus one asset.** She moved a paid private Instagram group (10,000 members in year one, no products at first) to Skool after merchant accounts kept freezing her ebook sales (about five blocked, roughly $30k held each) [~0:01:51 to 0:03:03, cues 37-61]. Day-one Skool contents: one ebook ($500 including community access) and herself [~0:03:03, cue 61; ~0:21:40, cue 433]. For about a year and a half the classroom was one module and one ebook [~0:29:29, cue 589]; it now has nine modules and about 40 videos [~0:23:29, cue 469].
2. **Migrate the existing audience in one push.** About 1,500 members joined within an hour when she moved platforms [~0:50:31, cue 1009]. Those who value you move immediately; the rest follow if you stay consistent.
3. **Build the product from member requests.** Every video or ebook began as a member ask; she kept members involved in organization and feedback [~0:04:15, cue 85; ~0:28:17, cue 565].
4. **Funnel: story/reel to ManyChat to link.** The ManyChat message answers the most frequent DM questions and lists everything included, so her about page can be three sentences and a stock image [~0:07:51 to 0:11:28, cues 157-229]. A follow-up nudge added later did nothing, so she runs none [~0:12:40 to 0:13:16, cues 253-265].
5. **Sell the outcome, not the inventory.** Posts show lifestyle and results, never feature lists; she segments the audience into three buyer types (interested in the skill, in her personality, in the lifestyle) and makes waves of content for each [~0:09:03 to 0:10:15 and 0:18:40 to 0:19:16, cues 181-205, 373-389].
6. **Content mechanics:** top two formats are Instagram stories (reviews screenshotted into a branded template with the link on every story) and trend-based 10-second reels with the CTA in the caption; one reel hit about 405K views and drove many members [~0:13:52 to 0:15:40, cues 277-313; ~0:38:30, cue 769]. No schedule or planning; the things she works least on perform best [~0:19:52, cue 397]. Her account is business-only, which cost followers but raised revenue [~0:16:16 to 0:17:28, cues 325-349].
7. **Conversion rate is irrelevant; traffic is the lever.** Hers is about 1.7% on 30,000 views; she posts every day including weekends and has never run ads [~0:39:06 to 0:40:18, cues 781-805; ~0:52:19, cue 1045].
8. **Remove free substitutes for access.** She stopped giving away lives and DMs on Instagram so the community is the only place to get her [~0:31:17 to 0:32:29, cues 625-649]. "Influencers treat their audience as the asset; owners at the top treat the community as the asset" (host framing) [~0:32:29, cue 649].
9. **TikTok Live (new, two weeks old on the call):** about 20 members from early sessions; a branded live layout, no teaching, share-screen tours of member wins, and "link in bio" in place of ManyChat [~0:33:05 to 0:36:42, cues 661-733]. TikTok pushes lives to strangers, unlike Instagram.
10. **Pricing:** no strategy by her own account. Annual $500, then $1,500, then three monthly tiers ($50, $100, $200 as stated), always tied to added value; she sometimes raises price when overwhelmed, and people still buy [~0:45:43 to 0:46:55, cues 913-937]. Tiers beat free trials: a free weekend gave about 300 to 350 signups but only about 40 converted [~0:47:31 to 0:48:43, cues 949-973].
11. **Delivery that retains:** daily live trading 9 a.m. Mon-Fri, treated like a stream (music, intro, personality), recorded into the classroom; every-other-Tuesday sessions; one-to-ones; games, giveaways, a 500-to-20K challenge, in-person meetup, a faith and mindset module and a "rich cousins" family culture [~0:21:40 to 0:27:41 and 0:40:18 to 0:44:30, cues 433-553, 805-889]. Engagement and retention correlate only about 50/50, but engaged members stay longer [~0:51:07, cue 1025].

**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md; lesson page classroom/100k-mo-interviews/03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md ("eBook and herself" launch, daily routine, TikTok Live to paid).

### Framework: Josh Madakor Operating Model (The Cyber Range)
**When to use:** Your product is hands-on, cost-bearing infrastructure plus community (labs, tools, simulations) in a job-outcome niche, or you have a modest audience and a reputation for accurate, useful content.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Climb the ladder of offers.** One-to-one hour slots sold out but turned into therapy sessions and capped income; an IT-entry course made about $10k in month one and about $40k in month two (best day $40k, best month a little over $100k); a cybersecurity course followed; then the community, because a one-time course cannot fund lifetime support [~0:03:00 to 0:05:55, cues 37-73].
2. **Product-led by outcomes and reputation.** Graduates who got jobs were interviewed on his channel, which sold the next offer; he credits a spotless reputation and accurate content for launches landing every time [~0:09:48, cue 121].
3. **Price from unit cost, then simplify.** The cyber range costs about $600/month even with zero users, and he feared heavy cloud use, so he launched around $384; measured spend turned out to be about $5 per member per month, so he cut to $97 to widen access and gather data, later $99 then $129. Regret: bouncing the price early; he would have stayed stable (and used $99 instead of $97). Announced increases create FOMO spikes that also make churn data noisy [~0:13:42 to 0:16:37, cues 169-205]. Average LTV is stated at over $800 [~0:06:53, cue 93].
4. **Traffic:** educational YouTube (typical videos around 2,000 views) with a gentle CTA near the end, a link in everything; other platforms kept alive for risk reduction by dedicated staff; Skool's network and Growth Boost (he runs 30%) feed most of the rest [~0:20:31 to 0:29:16, cues 253-361]. Fixing Growth Boost inputs (a spreadsheet of fixes generated from Skool's best-practice thread, cover image last) produced a visible traffic jump [~0:30:15, cue 373]. Instagram (about 20k), TikTok (about 14k) and LinkedIn (about 50k) contribute little [~0:28:18, cue 349].
5. **"Inside-out" content:** support answers for members become YouTube videos; project videos similar to what is inside the paid environment work well [~0:22:28 to 0:23:26, cues 277-289]. He sees the community, not the channel, as the asset [~0:23:26, cue 289].
6. **Team:** TikTok manager, Instagram manager, two YouTube editors, a finance officer, a threat-hunt engineer, one back-end developer, and an AI avatar tool for clips. He splits his own day into two big slots: community engagement and support first, then whatever is next; content suffers when engineering takes the second slot [~0:25:23 to 0:26:21, cues 313-325].
7. **Retention engine:** heavy, sustained activity (answering every post and every introduction) visibly lowers churn; Sarah-style personal Loom welcomes are cited by the hosts as the same idea [~0:32:12 to 0:34:08, cues 401-421]. Recurring events: Saturday live call, member-run open mic, a weekly threat-hunt contest with cash prizes (about $1,000, three times a month, moving to a participation-scaled prize capped near $2,000) [~0:38:02 to 0:40:57, cues 469-505].
8. **Classroom downsizing:** from about eight tiles to five or six (Start Here and onboarding, hands-on environment, internship and job-hunt) because bloat overwhelms [~0:18:34 and 0:41:56, cues 229, 517].
9. **Proof engine:** pinned posts every time a member gets a job, plus a spreadsheet of job outcomes (name, date, title, thread) that he mines with Claude for content; the about page uses an unedited explainer video, a classroom screenshot and as many wins as possible [~0:37:04 to 0:38:02 and 0:42:54 to 0:44:51, cues 457-469, 529-553].
10. **Mindset rules:** only two business outcomes matter: get customers, retain customers. "No zero days" (do something toward the goal every day) is a community slogan [~0:11:45, cue 145; ~0:46:48, cue 577]. Following the platform's own best-practice course for several hours a day beats improvisation [~0:45:49, cue 565]. Taking two years to build would have cost millions in opportunity cost, so move fast [~0:58:28, cue 721].

### Framework: James Lu Operating Model (SAT prep, the 1550 Formula)
**When to use:** Small or seasonal audience, young or low-budget buyers, an owner who dislikes content creation, or any niche where one great evergreen video can feed a paid program for years.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Pick one thing and stay.** After roughly six businesses in seven years he committed to SAT prep [~0:00:59 to 0:01:27, cues 25-37]. He first grew on TikTok but had no monetization; a free Skool group reached number one in discovery (about 10,000 members in two days) but did not convert to paid, and he would not run a free group again [~0:02:24 to 0:02:52 and 0:44:30, cues 61-73, 1129; ~0:54:55, cue 1393].
2. **One evergreen YouTube video moves the business.** A video that fixed a major SAT pain point, with the community link in the description, took MRR from about $5k to about $20k almost immediately at $99/mo [~0:03:49 to 0:04:46, cues 97-121]. Three videos built most of the community; he posts about two videos a month, uses no CTAs, and puts the community link first in the description [~0:23:13 to 0:24:38 and 0:56:20, cues 589-625, 1429].
3. **Offer = faster score gains plus getting past plateaus.** A flowchart built to train his tutors became the first paid course; the first paid modules were math, reading, grammar and the flowchart (four or five modules) [~0:19:26 to 0:19:54, cues 493-505; ~0:49:14, cue 1249]. Price $99/mo was chosen because the platform's challenge price limit was $100, with no further analysis [~0:19:54, cue 505]. The group title carries the outcome ("1550 formula").
4. **Free vs paid line:** YouTube teaches the concepts; the paid group handles plateaus, edge cases, troubleshooting and his self-built software [~0:14:42 to 0:16:07, cues 373-409].
5. **Software and AI as value.** Without coding experience he built a practice and diagnostic platform in about two weeks using AI, after third-party tools failed him; his regret is not doing it earlier instead of paying about $60 per student for another tool [~0:09:58 to 0:10:26 and 0:26:32 to 0:27:00, cues 253-265, 673-685]. A monthly AI scraper scores members' helpfulness and he pays cash to the top helpers, which created 24/7 support with replies in about 10 minutes [~0:11:23 to 0:12:20, cues 289-313]. Skool points alone were gamed, so he stopped relying on the leaderboard.
6. **Onboarding with zero ambiguity:** a Start Here post copied from Nick Saraev, a Start Here course, a 12-day daily-task schedule with a simple post each day (screenshot a result, choose A or B) so engagement exists before the group is large [~0:27:57 to 0:29:22 and 0:44:59 to 0:46:24, cues 709-745, 1141-1177]. The first video sets expectations: roughly seven hard weeks, so the wrong people self-select out [~0:37:53 to 0:38:50, cues 961-985].
7. **Resell three times:** sell on the video, again on the about page, again in onboarding so members confirm the decision, and then over-deliver beyond what was promised [~0:38:50 to 0:40:43, cues 985-1033].
8. **Video testimonials:** a 26-minute compilation at the top of the about page lifted conversion from 2.4% to 3.4% [~0:30:19, cue 769]. He DMs students, asks them to phone-record a rant, offers editing help, and about half say yes without any incentive [~0:31:44 to 0:32:41, cues 805-829]. Early on he used screenshots of TikTok comments as proof [~0:41:12 to 0:42:08, cues 1045-1069]. Recent score screenshots after each test date help.
9. **Solo capacity:** DMs about an hour a day, two weekly live office hours (one weekend morning, one weeknight) that run until every question is answered, sometimes six hours; recordings go in the classroom [~0:34:06 to 0:35:31, cues 865-901]. He diagnoses from his software dashboard and sends copy-paste instructions. He refunds fully when someone says it did not work [~0:35:59, cue 913]. He believes 80% or more of members skip tasks, and roughly 70% of office-hour problems trace to skipped steps [~0:36:56 to 0:37:53, cues 937-961].
10. **Seasonality handled with the annual plan:** retention was 98% over the summer, about 88% now, and may fall to about 30% in December; the annual plan is priced a bit under four months because that is the time to the next test date [~0:51:08 to 0:52:33, cues 1299-1331].
11. **High ticket tried and dropped:** he believes it could double MRR, but training staff to his standard was too costly; he moved to low-ticket self-paced instead [~0:47:21 to 0:48:46, cues 1201-1235].

### Framework: Samuel G Operating Model (Creative Owners Club)
**When to use:** You have real operating expertise in a high-stakes niche, can run live video, and want to build a distributed affiliate-streamer network that sells for you.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Seed from an existing list.** After years of free weekly Zoom classes (40 to 50 attendees), he put his course on Skool and emailed the list a "last Zoom" invitation; about 150 attended and about 70 joined that night [~0:05:40, cue 37].
2. **Go where strangers discover you and trust fast.** Ads and Instagram posts brought traffic without conversions; he went live on TikTok for 45 straight days, initially with 10 to 15 viewers, until the algorithm and his message clicked [~0:07:30, cue 49]. Plain language was the unlock (swap legal terms for how a hot dog vendor would say it).
3. **Build a repeatable live framework** (see the TikTok Live Machine below). Typical live: 15,000 to 20,000 people pass through, 20 to 30 join, record 97 on one stream [~0:09:21, cue 61].

### Framework: The TikTok Live Machine (Samuel G, Q&A plus the live example)
**When to use:** Your niche has an enticing "they lied to you / there is another way" angle, you can talk for an hour or more without a script, and you want paying members from strangers without existing followers. Do not use it if you cannot be on camera or sustain high energy.

**Steps/Template:**
1. **Talk to the platform, not the crowd.** Start immediately with no "waiting for five more people"; say clearly what the stream is about, who it helps and how, so the algorithm can route the right viewers. The platform reads words, logos on the wall, energy, volume and clarity [~0:13:01 to 0:20:23, cues 85-133 in the Q&A].
2. **Stand, move and make it fun.** He does not sit; energy matters more than wording; a game plays in the background because viewers called him Mario [~0:20:23 to 0:22:13, cues 133-145].
3. **Plain-language framework with five blocks** (reconstructed from the live example): (a) hook with a number that sounds impossible and chat prompts for yes/no answers (rates of 2.375% to 3.5% vs bank rates of 7%+) [~0:00:03 to 0:02:00, cues 1-37 in 07]; (b) live, replicable demo on a public tool (five steps on a property site, repeated for chat-requested cities) [~0:04:36 to 0:08:31, cues 85-157]; (c) objection list knocked down one by one with a diagram, a government form and a real contract as evidence [~0:10:28 to 0:31:57, cues 193-589]; (d) personal story and values ($12,500 seminar then a $30,000 hand-holding upsell, hot dog vendor, "I want to help regular people") [~1:08:25 to 1:16:53, cues 1261-1417]; (e) the invitation [~1:16:53 to 1:24:42, cues 1417-1561].
4. **Value first, no pitch until late.** The first hour contains no mention of the community; the invitation comes only after viewers ask for help ("who here wants me to hold your hand?") [~0:13:01 to 0:14:52 in the Q&A, cues 85-97; ~1:17:32, cue 1429 in 07].
5. **Make the offer small, scarce and fair.** In the example he asks whether a $1,000 fee for guided help would be fair (about 20 yeses), declines it, names $47 as an accountability price (tied to the last money in his pocket as a vendor), then drops it to $25 for 15 people, with a 30-second link window and first-come access [~1:20:09 to 1:24:03, cues 1477-1549]. The link is the TikTok Service Plus form; he repeatedly tells viewers where the orange button is and pins the link.
6. **Annual bundle on the spot:** $200 for the year (a $100 saving, "four months free") plus a premium bonus (introduction to a hard money lender, up to $1M proof-of-funds letter); monthly members can upgrade with a prorated credit and he demos the upgrade clicks live [~1:38:23 to 1:41:38 and 1:44:54, cues 1813-1873, 1933].
7. **Close the loop in the chat:** ask joiners to type their names, read the names aloud, and answer "when does it start" with a specific live schedule (five meetings a week, first class the next evening at 9 p.m. Eastern) [~1:36:26 to 1:37:44 and 1:43:35 to 1:44:14, cues 1777-1801, 1909-1923].
8. **Handle haters in public:** do not argue; ask the audience if they agree, let the crowd defend you, keep the flow, and have moderators mute or remove; skip off-topic questions that break the flow, and answer them inside the group [~0:16:42 to 0:19:35 and 0:47:58 to 0:50:50 in the Q&A, cues 109-121, 313-329; ~0:57:10 in the example].
9. **Starting rule:** the first three to five lives will be bad; go live without scripts, let questions shape your framework, then analyze and iterate [~0:38:46, cue 253 in the Q&A].
10. **Support content:** clip the lives and post them on TikTok with CTAs for supplementary traffic [~0:40:37, cue 265].

**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md (strategy and Q&A) and 07-samuel-g-tiktok-live-example.md (the full live, about 1 hour 55 minutes, with roughly 260 to 300 viewers mid-stream and about 150 at the end; 14 of the 15 spots had filled by roughly 1:52, cue 2077). Note: the example includes real-estate legal claims made on the stream; the framework is reusable, the claims are his own and are not endorsed here.

## Key Principles
- Test in the market, not in your head: ship a minimal, imperfect version, then ask the first 20 people what is wrong with it (Nick, Jade, James, Oscar all launched thin).
- Build one narrow, outcome-defined promise and repeat it in your bio, cover, description, ManyChat message and every video.
- Where possible, make the outcome boolean ("land the first client", "reach a score", "get a job") so proof and guarantees are crisp.
- The owner showing up daily is the biggest retention and conversion asset; schedule it, time-box it and use tools (voice-to-text, recorded answers to repeated questions) to keep it sustainable.
- Traffic volume matters more than percentage tweaks for a stable conversion rate of roughly 1.7% to 3.4% (Jade 1.7%, Oscar 2.1% to 3%, James 2.4% to 3.4%).
- Send traffic directly to the Skool about page; every extra funnel step costs conversion.
- Proof is the best about-page asset: collect testimonials constantly (ask by DM, make it phone-easy), pin job or income wins, and add recent results.
- Price low enough that the first 100 members join easily and raise later; if you raise, do it once with a stated trigger, because members compare baselines and dislike changes.
- Free things are used less; some payment creates accountability, which is why Jade, Samuel and Oscar avoid trials.
- Keep the classroom to a few obvious tiles and one numbered path; downsize bloated classrooms.
- Add leverage before you hit your capacity ceiling: coaches (Oscar), affiliates (Samuel), a helper-payout system (James), staff (Josh).
- Give refunds without a fight; it protects reputation and often produces a stronger testimonial later.
- Protect focus: Oscar rejects all other offers, James refuses a second product line, Josh warns that busywork feels productive but moves nothing.

## Decision Tools
- IF your audience is on YouTube and trusts long-form → THEN follow Nick or James: description link, 99% value, one hard pitch at the end of long videos, and evergreen videos that keep paying.
- IF you have a visual transformation or daily proof content → THEN follow Oscar: one before/after per day, volume over optimization, a single mission line.
- IF your following is on Instagram or TikTok and personality-led → THEN follow Jade: ManyChat keyword funnel, trend-based reels, lifestyle outcome, community-only access.
- IF you can speak live for an hour on a counterintuitive topic → THEN follow Samuel's live framework and train others to do it.
- IF your delivery has real marginal cost per member → THEN follow Josh: measure usage for a month before settling on price.
- IF your outcome is a yes/no event within a known timeframe → THEN write a boolean guarantee and track refund rate.
- IF you are launching in under a week and have nothing built → THEN ship a short numbered program, a weekly call on the calendar and a Start Here post; collect feedback from the first 20 members.
- IF your traffic is low → THEN raise content volume first (Oscar's doubling rule), not the funnel.
- IF your conversion rate is under 1% with high traffic → THEN check offer clarity, proof and price before the about-page design.
- IF members stop replying or leave early → THEN raise owner activity (answer every post), add a recurring event, and re-sell the decision in onboarding (James).
- IF you hit about 1,000 to 3,000 members and DMs overwhelm you → THEN add coaches, office hours or an incentivized helper system.
- IF you are tempted to add a free trial → THEN weigh Jade's 40 of about 300 conversion and Samuel's reversal, and use a low-priced tier instead.
- IF your product is seasonal → THEN create an annual plan sized to the season (James) and expect a December-style churn dip.
- IF you plan to raise price → THEN pick a member-count trigger, announce once, and accept noisy churn data (Nick, Josh).
- IF you are solo and capped on time → THEN time-box community work to about an hour a day and save repeated answers as reusable videos.

## Anti-Patterns
- Spending weeks on curriculum before any member sees it (every mentor launched imperfect).
- Selling inventory on social ("nine modules, 24-hour support") instead of the outcome and lifestyle (Jade).
- Bouncing prices or locking content phases after launch, which erodes trust (Oscar, Josh).
- Overbuilding the classroom until members cannot find anything (Nick, Josh, Oscar all downsized or warned against it).
- Using other people's communities to funnel their members to yours (Josh calls this a bannable offense).
- Treating a free trial as a growth hack; it yields cancellations and disengaged members (Jade, Samuel).
- Waiting for viewers or begging for follows on a live (Samuel).
- Arguing with haters instead of serving the live audience (Samuel).
- Promoting several offers at once; split attention dilutes the call to action (Oscar).
- Over-optimizing conversion percentage while traffic is small (Oscar, Jade).
- Copying a mentor's polished present state instead of their early sequence (Oscar: natural progression, change one thing at a time).
- Raising price too high too fast and losing the network effect (Nick's regret).
- Passively watching mentors' content without implementing (Josh).
- Taking all DMs yourself past the point where it harms output (Oscar, James); build leverage first.
- Assuming any one number here is a promise; all are self-reported and niche dependent.
