## Core Concept
A launch-ready Skool needs surprisingly little: a clear page, one welcoming "Start Here" post that points new members to the next step, a small classroom that mixes teaching, recorded calls, and resources, and a kickoff call on launch day. The lessons are blunt that the biggest mistake is spending weeks building a full course before anyone is in the room, because creators routinely guess wrong about what their audience wants. The recommended path is to ship a minimum viable community in roughly two to four hours, then build the classroom live with members, which also makes them feel like co-creators and tends to lower churn. Creators moving existing paying members from another platform have a separate, benefits-led playbook that treats the move as an upgrade and a reward.

## Frameworks

### Framework: The 6-Step Setup Sprint
**When to use:** Standing up a new Skool, or sanity-checking one that feels half-finished.
**Steps/Template:**
| # | Task | Output | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positioning, deliverables, pricing | One-line statement, deliverable list, price | Task 1 |
| 2 | About page | Landing page with video + text | Task 2 |
| 3 | Cover image and group description | Ad-ready cover plus one-liner | Task 3 |
| 4 | "Start Here" post | One welcome post asking for an introduction | Task 4 |
| 5 | Plan the classroom | MVP structure, not a full course | Task 5 |
| 6 | Schedule a kickoff call | Launch-day live event on the calendar | Task 6 |
- Total expected effort: roughly 2 to 4 hours.
- Completion check (the "Next Steps" lesson): positioning, deliverables and price decided; about page set up; description and cover uploaded; Start Here post written; classroom planned "without overthinking"; kickoff call scheduled.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/13-the-6-setup-steps.md and classroom/start-here/22-next-steps.md.

### Framework: Launch-First Mindset (the Member f1e9 Bult Case)
**When to use:** The creator is stalling on "I have no course yet", "my audience is too small", or "my offer isn't strong enough to charge $20".
**Steps/Template:**
1. Recognize the three common objections that delay launches: why pay if there is no course yet, is the offer strong enough, is the audience big enough.
2. Study the proof case: Member f1e9 Bult's The 1% Club launched with a single iPhone video of about one minute twenty, a short description, and a post on socials. No course, no PDF library, no polished sales video, no free trial. The commentary in the video reports about a 5% conversion on a paid group almost immediately and around 200 members in a week.
3. Draw the lesson: what was "inside" was the creator himself. People join for access, trust, and the sense of building something together, not for a content library.
4. Note the anecdote about asking members what they value most: live hang-outs and access ranked above content, since content is available free on social.
5. Compare with the failure mode: someone who spent eight hours shooting and editing an eight-minute classroom video before having traffic or revenue.
6. Adopt the loop: launch with a vision, deliver it, collect live feedback, build what members actually ask for. Building the wrong thing is the real waste.
7. Keep Nick Saraev's reframing in mind: the question is how to set up an 80%-right habit repeatable for a year, not how to get it 100% right today.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/11-it-s-really-that-simple.md with transcripts/start-here/11-it-s-really-that-simple.md (the captions run on a single timestamp, with the Member f1e9 Bult segment at roughly line 41-50 and the "overthinking" comment near line 81-103); classroom/start-here/12-launch-first-build-later.md. The lesson page tells members not to proceed without watching it.

### Framework: Nick Saraev's Zero-to-Twenty Launch Playbook
**When to use:** The creator wants a concrete, copyable sequence for the first days of a Skool.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Create a blank paid community and pay the platform fee, which acts as a pre-commitment (Nick says he did not actually want to run a community at that point).
2. Spend about 20 minutes on basic assets in Canva: a simple white-on-black logo and cover.
3. Record only the first day or two of the first program, around two hours in total, then call the classroom done for launch ("14-Day Agency" in his case).
4. Message about 20 of your best followers and people who had shown interest in paying. Make it personal and low-pressure: ask them to join, post an introduction, and say what they think. Offer something in return if they want it.
5. Seed the community with introductions so the first organic visitors see an active, capable group.
6. Make the group public and price it low to start (about $20 per month).
7. Add a weekly community call, and raise the price gradually and fairly as the recordings and value accumulate; he went on until reaching an early member cap around 500 before pausing to rethink strategy.
8. If roughly 30% of the basics exist, launch; do not wait for the rest.
- A parallel account from his Q&A: he DMed people on Twitter who had engaged with his posts, got 20 to 30 beta testers, asked what they thought, and most said parts of it were weak. He compiled the complaints, rebuilt the curriculum accordingly, then landed a first customer at a low price. He launched softly late at night so he would not be watching nervously, and put a monthly call in the calendar.
**Example from source:** transcripts/start-here/12-launch-first-build-later.md; transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [line ~60-100].

### Framework: Start Here Post Anatomy
**When to use:** Writing the first and only required post in a new community.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Greeting that restates the reason they are here.** One or two sentences of warmth tied to the community's promise.
2. **A call to introduce themselves.** The only required action. Provide a prompt so they know what to write.
3. **Optional onboarding steps** the group needs (where to go, what to watch, what to add to the calendar).
4. **Optional personal note** that explains why the community exists, written in the creator's voice.
- The lesson's three example screenshots show three flavors:
  - **The 1% Club (Member f1e9 Bult), minimalist:** a short welcome, the community idea in one line ("improve 1% each week"), then an introduction template: name, where you are from, the number-one area you are working on, one goal for the next 90 days, one fun fact, followed by a nudge to say hi to a couple of other members.
  - **Set The Standard (Chris Bumstead), navigational:** a title flagged "Read This First", a short map of channels (General, Wins, App, Ask The Coaches) with what each is for, then "your first 3 steps": introduce yourself, watch the Start Here module in the classroom, add the weekly call to your calendar. It closes with a brief personal belief about community.
  - **Team Relentless (a member James West), step-by-step:** four numbered steps: watch the welcome video in the Start Here classroom, post an introduction with fill-in-the-blank lines, reply to three to five other introductions, and download the weekly plan (with a note that a new workout drops each Sunday).
- Field update 2026-10-09: staff (Mark N.) says ask for introductions as a separate POST, not a comment, so intros get interaction and new members learn to post [community/questions/2026-10-08-rate-my-start-here-post-please.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/18-task-4-start-here-post.md; images classroom/images/start-here/18-task-4-start-here-post-1.png, -2.png, -3.png.

### Framework: Start Here Sequence and Sell-Three-Times
**When to use:** Designing what a member sees from the moment they pay until their first win, especially if churn is a concern.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **One obvious sequence.** Nick recommends a short numbered list of what everyone does first (watch this, find and like a post, introduce yourself) and notes Skool has a feature that surfaces such first steps. His start-here post explains the order, and the classroom starts with a "start here" tile.
2. **Clarity of next step at all times.** James Lu ($100k/mo) says he never wants a student to be uncertain; he runs a Start Here post and a Start Here course so the first click is always obvious and each piece shows how it adds up to the outcome.
3. **Expectation-setting video as the first lesson.** James tells new members the work is hard and will take about seven weeks, which filters for committed people. He borrowed the idea from a Hormozi-style top-1%-effort talk.
4. **Sell three times.** The first sale is the content that brought them in, the second is the about page, and the third is the onboarding module that reconfirms the decision, ideally surrounded by wins. Joining is not the same as staying.
5. **Copying is accepted.** James says he screenshotted Nick's welcome post and changed little; the structure is reusable.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [line ~924-952]; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [line ~709-745, ~963-1009].

### Framework: MVP Classroom (Three Uses)
**When to use:** Deciding what belongs in the classroom before launch.
**Steps/Template:**
| Use | What goes in | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Course content | Pre-recorded lessons | Prioritize teaching and value over production quality; no fancy editing. Upload an existing course if there is one; do not build one before launching if there is not. |
| 2. Recorded live calls | Replays of calls | Lets members who missed it catch up; builds the library without extra production. |
| 3. Extra resources | Worksheets, PDFs, spreadsheets, templates, workflow files | Downloadable assets. |
- Why building live works, per the lesson: it responds to specific member needs, and members feel like co-creators, which builds identity and lowers churn.
- The lesson gives screenshots for each use: a five-lesson "learn the moves" course (short step-by-step lessons with emoji titles), a "Live Calls (Recorded)" course listing dated calls, a Resources list of downloadable workflow files, and three full-grid classroom examples (see Classroom Shape Rules).
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/19-task-5-plan-your-classroom.md; images 19-task-5-plan-your-classroom-1.png through -6.png; classroom/IMAGE-NOTES-B.md (examples 5 and 6).

### Framework: Classroom Shape Rules (small, linear, obvious)
**When to use:** Laying out the classroom tiles.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Start with a tile that onboards**, then a "chapter one" or "step one". Josh Madakor says the simplest classrooms are the most intuitive.
2. **Roughly 3 to 6 tiles**. Josh recommends at least about three obvious ones that also teach how to use the community, and is cutting his own back from a bloated set to five or six because more feels overwhelming.
3. **Put resources in one place** rather than scattering them across many folders. Nick estimates most members absorb under 5% of the work in cluttered communities because they cannot find it.
4. **Order it so the path is linear.** Nick separates his program by month, and his main program literally by day.
5. **Reference layouts from the lesson:** Maker School's grid (Pre-Program, Resource Library, Automation Tutorials, then Month 1/2/3 with later months locked until earlier ones are done); an AI creator community's six-tile grid (First Day "Start Here", Chapter 1 Basics, Chapter 2 Image, Chapter 3 Video, Chapter 4 Workflow Breakdowns, Chapter 5 Monetization); a cycling community's six color-coded tiles (Start Here, Programs, Community, Toolkit, Support and FAQ, Academy).
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md [line ~220-245]; 01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [line ~781-891]; classroom/IMAGE-NOTES-B.md.

### Framework: Resource Library and Community Exclusives
**When to use:** The classroom needs substance after launch, or the owner is spending too long retyping answers.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Build from requests.** Jade began with an e-book she already had and added modules and videos as members asked ("we need a video on this"); she reached about 40 videos over time and keeps members involved in how it is organized.
2. **Record answers once.** Nick records a roughly 20-minute video whenever a question repeats, files it as a "community exclusive", and links to it. He says this cut daily management time from around 70 to 80 minutes to roughly 15 to 40.
3. **Offer reusable assets.** Templates, scripts, prompts, and skills (his include cold emails, proposals, and AI skills). A community member reported replacing a two-hour tutorial with a copy-and-paste prompt document and saw students get stuck far less.
4. **Archive guest sessions** by topic folders.
5. **Update modules** if the content is time-sensitive; Jade charged members to re-purchase a weekly-updated module list.
6. **Presence matters more than content.** Nick says much of the value is not the information but having the creator around.
**Example from source:** 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [line ~455-472, ~562-577]; 01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [line ~831-854]; community/wins/2026-10-07-ai-prompt-google-doc-for-students-goated.md.

### Framework: Kickoff Call
**When to use:** The day the group opens to the audience.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Schedule a live call on launch day (the lesson says to host it that day) and add it to the Skool calendar.
2. Set the tone for the culture and expectations.
3. Answer general questions live; this is also the first source of classroom ideas.
4. Record it, which doubles as the first recorded-call lesson.
5. Continue with a recurring cadence: Nick runs weekly calls; Samuel G runs five community meetings a week, with some onboarding calls handled by top affiliates; James Lu runs two weekly office-hours calls and posts replays in the classroom.
- The Platinum cohort's own kickoff is shown in a screenshot as a one-hour Skool Live event with a link and an "add to calendar" button, which is where members find such events.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/20-task-6-schedule-kickoff-call.md (+ image 20-task-6-schedule-kickoff-call-1.png, described in classroom/IMAGE-NOTES-B.md); 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [line ~868-874]; 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md [line ~340-342]. Platinum itself holds a weekly onboarding call every Tuesday in October [08-onboarding-call.md, line ~14-24].

### Framework: Existing-Platform Transition (7 steps)
**When to use:** Moving paying members from Kajabi, Circle, Thinkific, Patreon or similar to Skool.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Highlight the benefits.** Present Skool as an improvement (more engagement, live calls, leaderboards, challenges, more content), not as a platform change, which sounds dull.
2. **Show conviction.** Confidence that this is the better experience carries members.
3. **Offer an exclusive bonus** available only inside Skool: bonus training, office hours, templates, exclusive content.
4. **Give founders a discounted rate.** Announce the public launch date and public price, but give current subscribers a lower rate for about a week before going public.
5. **Set a clear deadline** for when the old community stops, and remind them several times.
6. **Handle payment smoothly.** Ask them to pay for the new Skool and refund their last payment on the old platform, so they receive extra time at no extra cost.
7. **For holdouts,** keep billing on the old platform and invite them free to the new one so the subscription is not lost entirely.
- Field update 2026-10-09: member migrations so far move a small share (about 50 of 1,000+ from Mighty Networks); staff advice is one audience, one outcome, one community, never one group with two paths [community/discussion/2026-10-08-moving-groups.md; community/questions/2026-10-08-ask-2-different-skools-or-combine-them.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/21-how-to-transtion-from-another-platform.md. Community members posted related scenarios without captured replies: a Kajabi owner repositioning during a move (community/discussion/2026-10-06-membership-repositioning-rebranding-question.md), a Patreon owner who moved a $20k+ MRR audience and reached about $5k MRR on Skool from YouTube alone (community/discussion/2026-10-05-hey-everyone-honored-to-be-here.md), a free group of 2,000 deciding whether to grandfather or go paid (community/questions/2026-10-06-2000-members-in-free-community-do-i-grandfather-them-or-let-.md), and a creator selling through Shopify wondering about recurring versus annual (community/questions/2026-10-07-annual-or-monthly.md).

### Framework: Existing-Skool Tune-Up Path
**When to use:** The member already has a live Skool before joining Platinum.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Still run through the course to check each best practice.
2. Instead of setting up a new group, tweak the about page, classroom, and cover image toward the 100k-earner patterns.
3. The end goal stays the same: add the milestone MRR or be removed.
4. Do not skip the Mindset section, since the common failure is spending two to three weeks polishing and then discovering the audience wanted something else.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/08-onboarding-call.md [line ~516-557].

### Framework: Launch Timing and the Platinum Clock
**When to use:** Deciding how fast to go live.
**Steps/Template:**
- October is a setup-and-launch window. The milestone timer starts November 1; the first milestone is adding $1,000 MRR within 30 days of that, with $5,000 after 60 days (as described on the call). The community post on rankings carries the formal rules.
- Rankings count MRR growth from October 1, so launching earlier gets a head start; waiting until November leaves less time.
- The speaker's own advice: set up as soon as possible, because many people underestimate how long hitting a milestone takes.
- Mountain analogy: focus on the next base camp rather than the summit.
- Only recurring revenue (monthly and annual) counts; one-time payments do not. Annual plans are divided by 12; a high-ticket tier counts toward the board.
**Example from source:** 08-onboarding-call.md [line ~176-300]; community/updates/2026-09-25-welcome-to-skool-platinum-what-to-do-next.md (first steps: watch onboarding video, create the Skool, work through the course, launch). Community posts show members launching the day they were accepted (e.g., community/discussion/2026-10-06-accepted-yesterday-launched-today.md: "make it exist then make it better").

### Framework: Plan and Fee Choice
**When to use:** Picking the Skool subscription tier at setup.
**Steps/Template:** The call compares the lower-cost plan with the higher-fee structure: transaction fees are about 10% versus 2.9%, and break-even is around $1,300 MRR. Below that, the lower-priced plan costs less; above it, the plan with the lower fee leaves more take-home. Plans can be changed at any time.
**Example from source:** 08-onboarding-call.md [line ~417-447].

### Framework: Early-Member Retention Notes
**When to use:** Early members are joining but not engaging or are asking to cancel.
**Steps/Template:**
- Josh Madakor notes churn visibly falls when he responds to nearly everyone consistently, not for one day.
- Samuel G: many complaints come from members who never opened the classroom or answered outreach. His team calls, offers to restart together, and walks them through the first steps. He has run free trials briefly and found people invest less attention when they have paid nothing.
- Oscar Patel added levels and phases early to keep churn from being high.
- James Lu says most student problems trace back to not following what the course said.
**Example from source:** 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md [line ~402-406]; 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md [line ~339-374]; 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [line ~61-66]; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [line ~958-962].

## Key Principles
- Launching is a skill practiced by doing; most people only watch from the sidelines.
- What members are buying is access to the creator, trust, and a group on a journey together, more than a content library.
- Guessing what to build is usually wrong, and live feedback is cheaper than wrong guesses.
- One Start Here post is enough to open: greet, ask for an introduction, add any steps the group needs.
- A first-action sequence that is visible, numbered, and short reduces confusion and improves uptake of everything else.
- The classroom can be small. Three to six obvious tiles beats a sprawl.
- Simple course videos are acceptable; value matters more than production.
- Recorded calls and resource files count as classroom content and take little extra effort.
- Presence in the group retains members; the owner showing up daily matters more than extra content.
- Personal outreach to a handful of followers is a workable seeding method for the first twenty members.
- Price low and increase with value, gradually and fairly.
- Calling out how hard the work is can attract the right members.
- When moving paying members, framing the shift as a reward with a deadline and a discount converts better than announcing a platform change.
- Pre-committing (paying the fee, scheduling the call, telling people) removes the "not ready" excuse.
- Everything can be fixed live: "test in the market, not in your mind" (Nick, quoted on the onboarding call).

## Templates and Fill-Ins

### Start Here post
```
Title: Welcome to [Community name]! (Start Here)

I'm so glad you're here. [Community] exists to help [specific person] [specific outcome].

Your first [3] steps:
1. Introduce yourself below. Share: your name, where you're from, your #1 focus right now, one goal for the next [90 days], and one fun fact.
2. Watch the [Start Here tile] in the Classroom ([length]).
3. Add our [weekly call - day/time] to your calendar.

Where to go:
- [Channel 1] - [what it's for]
- [Channel 2] - [what it's for]
- [Channel 3] - [what it's for]

Then reply to [2-3] other introductions. This is a community, not just a course.

[One-line personal note: why I built this / what I believe]
See you in the [first call / community].
```

### MVP classroom outline (3 uses)
```
Tile 1: START HERE - [welcome video + how this works]  (cover: [____])
Tile 2: [Core program or Chapter 1] - [first 1-3 lessons only; label "more coming as we build together"]
Tile 3: Live Calls (Recorded) - [Call #1 date/topic placeholder]
Tile 4: Resources - [template / worksheet / prompt doc 1], [#2], [#3]
(Optional) Tile 5: [Extra module requested by members once launch asks]
Build rule: add lessons weekly in response to [member questions logged in ___]
Lesson format: title with emoji, 3-8 minute video, 2-line description
```

### Kickoff call agenda
```
Date / time / link: [______] (Skool Live, added to calendar, recorded)
1. Welcome and why this community exists - [3 min]
2. What the next [30/60/90] days look like - [5 min]
3. Walk through: Start Here, classroom, calendar - [5 min]
4. Member introductions or hot-seat - [10 min]
5. Q&A: "What's the one thing you're stuck on?" - [15 min]
6. Next steps and ask: introduce yourself, [first action] - [3 min]
7. Cadence announcement: weekly call every [day/time]
Capture afterward: top [5] questions asked -> next classroom lessons
```

### Founder-discount transition announcement
```
Subject/hook: [Community] is moving to [new home] - and you get the best deal.
What's better there: [engagement], [live calls], [challenge/leaderboard], [new content].
Exclusive for current members: [bonus].
Founding rate for you: [$__]/mo, available until [date], then the public price is [$__].
Old platform closes: [date]. Reminders: [dates].
How to switch: join here [link], then reply and I'll refund your last payment on [old platform].
Not ready? [Keep billing on old platform; free invite to new one] - [terms].
```

## Decision Tools
- IF the creator has no course THEN launch with a small classroom (start here + first lessons + calls + resources) and build the rest from member questions.
- IF the creator already has a course THEN upload it, but still keep the structure to a few clear tiles.
- IF the group is still unlaunched and the classroom is "almost ready" THEN set a short deadline (a day or two), launch, and finish in public.
- IF the creator worries the offer is not strong enough to charge THEN start low (Nick began around $20) and raise gradually as value grows.
- IF the creator has zero audience THEN message a small set of warm contacts personally and ask for an introduction and feedback.
- IF the community feels empty THEN seed it with introduction prompts and replies from the owner so early visitors see activity.
- IF new members seem lost THEN add a numbered first-steps list and a Start Here tile; reduce the number of top-level folders.
- IF members repeat the same question THEN record one answer and link it.
- IF existing members are on another platform THEN use the seven-step transition: benefits, conviction, bonus, founder rate, deadline, refund trick, free invite for holdouts.
- IF the creator already runs a Skool THEN keep the course walkthrough but spend the time on tweaks to the page, classroom, and cover.
- IF MRR is under about $1,300 THEN the lower-cost plan likely leaves more profit; IF above THEN the plan with the 2.9% fee likely does.
- IF unsure whether to set up before November 1 THEN launching earlier gives more runway and an earlier rank position.
- IF a free trial is under consideration THEN note Samuel G's observation that unpaid members engage less.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Months-long solo course creation before any members.** The most-repeated warning: creators guess wrong, and the work is wasted.
- **Over-optimizing every sentence of copy for two to three weeks.** It delays the feedback that actually improves the offer.
- **Heavy production on the first classroom video.** The eight-hour edit with no traffic is the cautionary example.
- **A sprawling classroom** where resources are scattered and the Start Here tile gets buried.
- **No clear first step.** Without one, members drift and churn.
- **Treating the move from another platform as a "platform change" announcement.** It sounds like a chore; a benefits-led upgrade with a deadline is more persuasive.
- **Open-ended migration.** Without a stop date or a reason to act now, members postpone indefinitely.
- **Waiting to be "ready" before the kickoff call.** The call is part of how the creator finds out what to build.
- **Skipping the Mindset lessons.** The onboarding call says people who skip them tend to repeat the over-prepare cycle.
- **Letting the group sit empty.** Early introductions and owner replies create the social proof the first organic visitor sees.
- **Relying on free trials by default.** Unpaid members in Samuel G's groups engaged less and declined at billing.
- **Raising price too fast.** Nick Saraev says that on reflection his own price climbed higher than much of his audience could afford, and a raised price cannot easily be walked back; existing members stay locked at their old rate.
