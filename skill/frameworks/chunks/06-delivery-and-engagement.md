## Core Concept
After launch, the Delivery folder reduces everything to one habit: show up every day. Daily presence builds personal relationships (members stay), reveals what members need (the offer improves), and produces visible results (proof and conversion improve). The owner is both the model of correct behavior and the person who rewards it, so daily DMs, replies, a recurring daily post, a weekly live call, and three deliberate phases of onboarding all serve a single end: the member's identity shifts from visitor to someone who sees themselves as part of the community. The mentors in the interviews differ on style (Nick answers posts at dawn, Jade goes live at 9 a.m., Oscar installs coaches) but agree on daily presence and on keeping things simple.

## Frameworks

### Framework: Show Up Every Day
**When to use:** Setting the baseline operating rhythm, and deciding what to protect when time is tight.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Treat daily presence as the one delivery job that matters (lesson 31 states it directly).
2. Expect three payoffs: relationships that prevent churn, deeper insight that improves the offer, and member results that strengthen proof and conversion.
3. Remember prospects can see the member activity heatmap. A fully green year signals a living group; a mostly grey one with scattered squares signals an absent owner.
4. Add the streak signal: Nick's interviewer notes a fire emoji beside a name appears after 30 consecutive active days.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/31-big-idea.md, with the two heatmap screenshots (good: every day filled; bad: sparse, per IMAGE-NOTES-B.md). The lesson's challenge line asks why members would show up if the owner will not. Nick Saraev says he missed only five or six days in a year and uses the gym analogy: seeing the same faces daily builds relationships through sheer repetition; when the owner is present, others copy the behavior and begin answering questions (transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, management section). Jade also says she posts her community every day, weekends included (03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md).

### Framework: Posting as Model and Reward (Hormozi's 5-Post Week)
**When to use:** Planning what the owner posts so the community learns the behavior you want.
**Steps/Template:**
- Posting has two jobs: model the correct behavior, and reward people for doing it. The owner is the standard bearer.
- Define the wanted member behaviors: valuable comment responses (support does not all come from you), valuable posts (content does not all come from you), and executing to get results (proof the product works).
- Then build a week of posts that highlight each:
| Slot | Post | Purpose / CTA |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement post (workstation-style, easy to join) | Lowers the bar to participate |
| 2 | Top Posts round-up (3 best of the week), alternating with Top Responses (3-5 best comment replies) | Rewards good posts or good answers; ask for favorites or to thank helpers |
| 3 | Top Wins round-up (3 biggest) | Shows results; ask members to drop theirs |
| 4 | Value add (take a top recent short from YouTube, put it into a post with a question) | Delivers teaching plus a conversation starter |
| 5 | Call recap / recording | Spreads the call's value to those who missed it |
- Scale: Hormozi himself posts about five times a week. With a small group, 2-3 posts a week avoids looking aggressive, and the five concepts can stretch over two weeks. Alternatively post less and pin the best posts, rotating the pin daily, which gives seven shout-out chances and removes one weekly post.
- He closes by saying it is a starting point, not a rulebook.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/32-hormozi-s-posting-advice.md. The lesson is three screenshots (transcribed in classroom/IMAGE-NOTES-B.md under 32-hormozi-s-posting-advice-1 to -3) of a Hormozi Skool post and a sample calendar.

### Framework: Founder Week Time Blocks
**When to use:** Fitting delivery around the real job of driving traffic.
**Steps/Template:** The calendar screenshot in lesson 32 shows a suggested week: a one-hour Post block at 9-10am each working day (Sunday through Thursday in the sample), a live Call block (Sunday 10-11:30am), and the rest of the workday (roughly 10am to 5pm, or 11:30am on Sunday) given to Traffic. Friday and Saturday are blank.
**Example from source:** classroom/images/start-here/32-hormozi-s-posting-advice-1.png via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md. Nick describes the same rhythm: by about 6:45 a.m. he has replied to dozens of posts, so it feels like it costs no work time. Josh Madakor notes that doing community engagement and engineering work in the same slot is nearly impossible, so he reserves different slots (04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md).

### Framework: Daily Owner Flow
**When to use:** Every login. The lesson calls this the minimum.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Clear DMs.** Members ask questions and respond to auto-DMs. Answer, then engage: ask questions and get to know them. A simple emoji or gif still makes someone feel heard. Personal relationships form in private, and relationships reduce churn.
2. **Respond to community posts.** Scan what was posted and who replied. Members state exactly what confuses them and what they want. If many people cannot find the starting point, fix onboarding; if many struggle with one thing, make a module for it. It is fine, even good, not to be the first responder, because leaving room lets members answer each other and reduces dependence on you.
3. **Make a daily post.** Pick one of five formats (below) and keep the time and style consistent.
- Keep building after launch: the lesson contrasts a 100-video library where only ten videos matter to a member with a community where the owner builds a new module the following week to solve a stated problem.
- Sam Ovens (Skool's CEO) is held up as a model for daily touchpoints: short replies like "nice work" and quick answers in member threads (screenshots in IMAGE-NOTES-B.md under 33-daily-flow-1 and -2).
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/33-daily-flow.md. Nick Saraev responds to roughly 75% to 100% of posts each morning; when someone else already answered he adds a thumbs-up or a gif, and he sets one improvement per day for the community, such as adding his target keyword to his group title to move up from page two to page one of Skool search. He also records a short "community exclusive" video the first time a question repeats and links it afterward, which he says cut daily management from about 70-80 minutes to 15-40, and he dictates replies with a voice transcription tool because speaking runs about 150 words a minute versus 30-50 typing (01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md).

### Framework: Daily Post Formats
**When to use:** Choosing the recurring post that gives members a predictable habit.
**Steps/Template:** Five formats from the lesson:
1. Engagement post: simple and fun to join.
2. Top Posts round-up: screenshot the three best of the week.
3. Top Wins round-up: the three biggest wins of the week.
4. Value add: a top-performing recent piece of content turned into a post with a question.
5. Call recap: key takeaways, a discussion prompt, and the recording.
Two owner examples: Mikey Caloca's "Good Morning" post lists upcoming live calls, celebrates wins, wishes members happy birthday, and shares updates, plus a video update (image: a "Morning Posts" entry announcing that night's hot seat and a sign-up form). Alan Powell's "Dear Awesomes Daily" carries a numbered day count, a question of the day, a short video (about 16 minutes in the screenshot), community updates, and a daily focus. A simple default formula: recap the last 24 hours, spotlight wins, and tease what is next (calls, giveaways, challenges).
- Pro tip from the lesson: include a short video of yourself. Members who see you daily build a relationship and churn less.
- Cadence reference: Skool's own Skoolers group publishes a weekly "Skool News" that has become a weekly habit for members (some stay up late to watch it live), but for a new group the lesson says to post daily.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/33-daily-flow.md, images 33-daily-flow-4 and -5 (via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md).

### Framework: Live-Call Cadence and Formats
**When to use:** Scheduling and running calls that create a weekly rhythm.
**Steps/Template:**
- Hold at least one call a week. Populate the calendar three to four months ahead even if you are unsure; an empty calendar sends visitors away. You can edit later.
- Calls build relationships, strengthen the community, and reduce churn.
- Seven format ideas: Q&A; Coffee Chat; Guest Speaker; Workshop (build live); Hot Seats (live feedback on member work); Implementation Session (everyone leaves with something done); Masterclass (deep dive on one topic).
- Pro tip: run a weekly giveaway. Members share a win or write a post to enter; on the call, screenshare a spinning-wheel tool to choose a winner. Prizes in the lesson include a gift card, three free months of access, or 1:1 time with you.
- Not sure what to cover? Ask members; the lesson shows a poll post with options such as Q&A, hangout, guest speaker, and wine night.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/34-live-calls.md and image 34-live-calls-1 (poll composer, via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md). Mentors' calendars: Nick holds one weekly one-hour call and archives them in a resource library; Josh does a Saturday 9 a.m. Pacific live, an open-mic night run by a member, and a week-long threat-hunt contest with cash prizes; Jade goes live Monday to Friday at 9 a.m.; Oscar runs a Sunday live; James Lu runs two weekly office hours and does not end them until everyone's questions are answered (once past 2 a.m.); Samuel G runs five Zoom calls a week, some led by top affiliates, including onboarding calls (transcripts 01, 04, 03, 02, 05, 06 in transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/). Community: a member asked whether Call or Webinar mode suits interview-format lives (community/questions/2026-10-04-call-or-webinar-for-interviews.md; no answer captured), and another asked if Platinum calls are recorded (community/questions/2026-10-06-are-the-calls-recorded.md).

### Framework: 3 Phases of Engagement
**When to use:** Walking a new signup from stranger to committed member. The goal stated in the lesson is identification: when a member aligns their identity with the community, they do not churn.
**Steps/Template:**
| Phase | Window | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | Week 1 | Initial DM (welcome, learn who they are); kickoff call 24-48 hours after launch to set the tone; welcome thread asking each new member for a short intro; personally tag new signups in the welcome post so they feel seen |
| 2. Trust and daily buy-in | Weeks 2-4 | Daily update or recap for predictable cadence; spotlight posts that pin a member win or insight (do not use pins only for logistics); accountability pairs by tagging members two at a time; check-in messages to members who went quiet; redirect private wins/questions into public posts so the group benefits |
| 3. Identity and culture | Week 4 onward | Promote trusted power users to moderators; lean into shared humor and inside jokes |
- The cumulative result is an "identity shift" from visitor to member.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/35-3-phases-of-engagement.md. Josh Madakor welcomes people and replies to every intro thread, calling heavy activity a churn-reduction engine that has to be sustained for a long time to show (04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md); he also pins job-win posts and keeps a spreadsheet of wins to turn into content, and mentions another owner sending personalized Loom videos to each new joiner. James Lu's Start Here post tells members up front that the program is hard, which sets expectations (05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md).

### Framework: High-Engagement Post Bank
**When to use:** Choosing prompts when you need a post fast. The lesson says these came from analyzing top communities.
**Steps/Template:** Four categories, grouped as in the lesson:
| Category | Ideas |
|---|---|
| Getting started / networking | Introduce yourself; share your socials; introduce yourself with workspace photo and a fun fact; where are you from (connect locally); kickoff thread to share a commitment, six-month goal, and an accountability partner |
| Discussion prompting | Your first time using [niche]; Do Hard Things (health goal); biggest lesson learned; biggest win this week; one tool you cannot live without; advice for beginners |
| Challenge announcements | Build challenge of the week (submit by a deadline, winner gets a 1:1 call and lifetime access); Feedback Friday (give feedback to at least two members); Speed Challenge (30 minutes, random winner gets a free month); Before and After Challenge (best transformation featured on the next call); Community Vote Challenge (likes decide the winner) |
| Miscellaneous | Biggest mistake; Build in Public (reply to your own comment on completion); $100 challenge (what would you buy); unpopular opinion; myths that need to die; finish the sentence ("The moment everything changed for me was when ____") |
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/36-bonus-high-engagement-post-ideas.md. In the community feed, the "share a pic of your workstation" thread (community/discussion/2026-10-05-share-a-pic-of-your-workstation.md) is an example of the easy-to-join engagement prompt.

### Framework: Incentive Mechanics (Giveaways, Challenges, Raffles)
**When to use:** When people read but do not post, or when you need wins to surface.
**Steps/Template:**
- Attach a prize to the behavior you want, then run the draw on a live call.
- Member 953e had trouble getting members to post deals; he announced that anyone posting a verified deal enters a monthly raffle (AirPods), with a trip to his office every six months, drawn live with a spin wheel. People who had never posted started sharing immediately. He asked whether this conflicts with Skool's terms, and no answer was captured (community/learnings/2026-10-07-engagement-boost.md).
- Jade makes learning fun with games, meetups, giveaways, and a challenge; she says it is the owner's job not to make learning boring (03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md).
- Josh's weekly threat hunt gives away cash prizes and recurs, so members have a standing activity.
- A high-ticket variation: Member 452e seeded a $500/month offer at the end of a weekly mastermind call, and five of about 30 live attendees (out of 230 members) joined, adding $2,401 MRR in an hour; he reports no complaints (community/wins/2026-10-06-i-added-2401-mrr-in-60-mins.md).

### Framework: Retention Levers from the Mentors
**When to use:** After engagement basics are in place and you want to reduce cancellations.
**Steps/Template:**
| Lever | Mentor / source | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-simple Start Here sequence | Nick, James Lu | A first post that lists numbered steps; start-here classroom; no hundred folders. Nick says most communities bury members' work so they use under 5% of it. James aims for no uncertainty about the next step and explains how the pieces connect to the goal |
| Phases and levels | Oscar | Unlock the simplest, most important material first; layer complexity so churn is not high |
| Access to the owner, scaled | Oscar | Past about 3,000 members he added coaches; the welcome message directs people to a coach and to the weekly live; he still receives roughly 15 messages a day |
| Recurring standing activity | Josh | Weekly contests, SOC work, and open mic keep members busy indefinitely |
| Identity and "no zero days" | Josh | Members repeat the phrase; he is considering a branded cap for members who stay over a year |
| Paid-only, no free trial | Samuel G | Free trials led to people not opening the classroom; a small payment ($25 in his case) creates commitment |
| Personal call when someone is angry | Samuel G | He phones the member, finds they never opened the classroom, and resets the start together; refunds without argument if needed |
| Video testimonials as re-selling | James Lu | Ask each success for a short video; his about page opens with a roughly 26-minute compilation; he also treats the onboarding module as a third sale |
| Annual plan matched to seasonality | James Lu | Annual priced slightly under four months because SAT prep runs about four months; retention 98% over summer, around 88% later, and large drops after a deadline month |
| Early forced engagement | James Lu | Assignment submissions early got his free group to the top of discovery in about a week |
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md, 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md, 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md, 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md. Jade's headline numbers: 88% engagement and 77% retention, and she estimates engaged members stay longer but says about half of people never speak yet stay for years (03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md).

### Framework: Churn Benchmarks and Diagnosis
**When to use:** Interpreting a monthly churn percentage and deciding what to examine.
**Steps/Template:**
- Benchmarks from Sam Ovens, reproduced in lesson screenshot 33-daily-flow-3 (via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md): amazing 2-9%, good 10-19%, average 20%, bad 21-30%. Lower is better; subtract from 100% to read retention.
- Typical community-reported churn patterns, with no answers captured in the export:
| Post | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Weird churn problem (Member 2875 Tripkovic) | 22% monthly churn; about 95% of cancellers say they love the community but lack time and will return |
| Silent churns (Member 61ab) | About 100 paying members from an early-bird launch; cancellers who answered gave good reasons, but silent ones never participated at all |
| Churn question (Member 6397) | Asks for case studies of what reduced cancellations |
| Tips for engagement (Member 7644) | 500-member group mostly grandfathered from free resources; only two or three people engage; asks about removing inactive members |
- Mentor-based diagnosis: members who never opened the classroom, never replied to outreach, or never posted are an onboarding problem (Samuel G's anecdote of a member ignoring 17 messages); members who love it but lack time suggest annual plans or seasonality (James Lu's pricing); an early-bird launch crowd may begin cancelling after the first month (the pattern Member 61ab describes).

## Key Principles
- Showing up daily is the delivery strategy; everything else is an arrangement of it.
- The owner models and rewards; members imitate what gets recognition.
- Do DMs first. Private conversations build the strongest relationships, and a single emoji still counts.
- Read posts like market research: confusion points to onboarding fixes, repeated struggles to new modules.
- Give the community room: replying to every post immediately trains dependency.
- Make a predictable daily post so members form a habit.
- Include face time: a short video each day makes you familiar and cuts churn.
- A call calendar populated three to four months ahead tells visitors the community is alive.
- Ask members what the next call should be; polls double as engagement.
- Pin wins and insights, not only logistics.
- Tag new signups by name in the welcome post; being seen is a retention move.
- Redirect private wins into public threads so others benefit.
- Promote reliable members to moderators once the culture is established.
- Simplify onboarding to a short numbered sequence; simplicity was the common thread across mentors.
- Incentives (raffles, giveaways, challenges) pull silent members into posting.
- Keep building in response to what members say, so the product always seems tailored to them.

## Templates and Fill-Ins

### Daily owner flow (morning checklist)
```
[ ] Clear DMs: answer, then ask one personal question or send an emoji/gif
[ ] Read and reply to community posts from the last 24 hours (leave room for members to answer first)
[ ] Note the top 1-3 confusion points: [_______________]  -> fix in onboarding or add a module: [_______________]
[ ] Publish today's post (format: [engagement / top posts / top wins / value add / call recap])
[ ] Check on members who went quiet: [names]
[ ] One small improvement today: [title keyword / about-page line / welcome page / ______]
```

### Daily "good morning" post
```
[Day name / date / day count: Day ___]
Good morning [community nickname]!
Today: [live call at ___ about ______] [challenge deadline] [giveaway draw]
Win shout-out: [member] just [result].
Birthday / milestone: [member].
Question of the day: [_______________]?
Short video: [1-3 minute update recorded today]
```

### Weekly 5-post plan (Hormozi-style)
```
Mon  Engagement post: [low-barrier prompt about ______]
Tue  Top Posts (or Top Responses) round-up: [3 screenshots] - "Drop your favorite below"
Wed  Value add: [top recent short, as a post with the question ______]
Thu  Top Wins round-up: [3 wins] - "Drop yours below"
Fri  Call recap: [3 takeaways + recording link + discussion question]
(Small group: spread these over two weeks, or pin and rotate the best posts daily)
```

### Welcome post and DM
```
DM: Hey [name], welcome to [community]! What brought you here and what are you hoping to get in the first 30 days?
Welcome post: Welcome [@[handle] member 1], [@[handle] member 2], [@[handle] member 3]! Introduce yourself with where you are from, what you want to achieve, and [one fun detail].
Kickoff call (24-48 hours after launch): [date/time], topics: [___], bring: [___]
```

### Accountability pairs post
```
Accountability pairs for the next [number] weeks. I am tagging you in twos:
[@A + @B], [@C + @D], [@E + @F]
Message your partner today, share one goal for the week, and check in on [day].
Post your update in the comments by [deadline].
```

### Live call agenda (60 minutes)
```
0:00 Welcome and wins shout-outs (spin the wheel for [prize])
0:10 Format segment: [Q&A / hot seats / workshop / masterclass on ______]
0:45 Implementation: each person commits to [one action] this week
0:55 Announce next call [date], theme [_____], and challenge [_____]
Afterward: post recap + recording, tag attendees, thank helpful members
```

### Call-format poll
```
Next live call: which do you want?
Options: Q&A / Coffee hangout / Guest speaker / Hot seats / [your idea]
Comment something new if you do not see what you want.
```

### Engagement post formulas
```
Intro: Introduce yourself: where you are from, what you want to achieve in [community], and one fun fact.
Discussion: What is the one lesson you wish you had learned sooner in [niche]? Share the advice that would have saved you time, money, or frustration.
Win: Biggest win this week, big or small? Celebrate it below.
Challenge: This week's challenge: [task]. Submit in the comments by [day/time]. Winner receives [prize].
Feedback Friday: Post your [project]. Give feedback to at least two others. I will review my favorites on [call].
Finish the sentence: "The moment everything changed for me was when ________."
```

### Spotlight pin
```
[Member] just [specific result]. Here is how they did it: [two or three bullets]. Take a look and drop your own win below.
```

### Check-in message to a quiet member
```
Hey [name], noticed we have not seen you in [community] for a bit. How are things going? What is the biggest thing in the way right now? Happy to point you to [resource] or help you pick a first step.
```

## Decision Tools
- IF your group has fewer than roughly 50 posts a week, THEN reduce owner posts to 2-3 per week (or pin and rotate) so the feed does not feel aggressive, per the Hormozi postscript.
- IF several members ask the same question, THEN record a short answer video or a new module and link it (Nick's community exclusive).
- IF many members are lost at the start, THEN rewrite the Start Here sequence into numbered steps.
- IF the calendar is empty past next month, THEN schedule recurring calls for the next three to four months before doing anything else.
- IF you are unsure what to run on a call, THEN poll the members.
- IF members read but never post, THEN attach a prize or a challenge deadline to posting.
- IF a member goes quiet, THEN send a check-in message; if they respond privately with a win, ask to share it publicly.
- IF a member cancels with "love it but no time," THEN consider an annual option or a lighter track.
- IF a member is angry about not receiving what was promised, THEN call them, open the classroom with them, and restart together.
- IF the owner reaches several thousand members, THEN add coaches and point the welcome message to them while keeping a weekly live (Oscar).
- IF monthly churn is over 20%, THEN you are average-to-bad by Sam Ovens's bands; look first at onboarding and week-one activity.
- IF engagement concentrates in two or three people, THEN rotate prompts, tag specific members, and use accountability pairs to widen participation.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Sporadic activity:** the heatmap makes absence visible to prospects and members.
- **Answering every post instantly:** it crowds out peers and creates dependency.
- **Pinned posts used only for logistics:** wastes the reward function.
- **Ignoring DMs:** you lose the relationship layer where retention is built.
- **An empty or short calendar:** new visitors read it as a dormant group.
- **Starting with a pile of polished modules:** the lesson prefers build-in-response to member statements.
- **Burying resources in many folders:** Nick says most members consume under 5% of what is there.
- **Free trials without commitment:** Samuel G found members never opened the classroom.
- **Treating private wins as private:** the group misses the proof and the reward loop.
- **Assuming silent members will tell you why they leave:** the silent-churn thread shows the non-responders are the ones who never engaged; reach them in week one instead.
- **Counting a prize raffle as a substitute for real value:** the sources use incentives to start behavior, then rely on relationships and results to keep it.
