## Core Concept
The about page is the landing page every piece of content points at, and it has exactly one job: persuade a stranger to join by answering what the community is, who it is for, and why joining beats not joining. It does that with a short, personality-forward video, a scannable text block, and proof, never with biography, clever slogans, or walls of text. The cover image is the same job compressed into a single glance: Skool reuses it as the first ad for your group, so a viewer should grasp the theme and the benefit within one to two seconds. The $100k mentors add a useful counterweight: the page only has to close people who are already warm, so the work done before the click (content, DMs, live streams) usually matters more than polishing the page itself.

## Frameworks

### Framework: The What / Who / Why Test
**When to use:** Before writing or rewriting any about-page copy, or when auditing an existing page that feels like it is talking a lot but not selling.
**Steps/Template:**
| Question | What a visitor must be able to say after reading | Where it usually lives |
|---|---|---|
| What is this community? | "It is a [format] where I get [deliverables]" | Opening line + what's-inside list |
| Who is it for? | "That is me: [specific person, stage, situation]" | Opening line, pain bullets, or video intro |
| Why should I join? | "It gets me [specific outcome], and others got it too" | Outcome line, proof, guarantee, urgency |
- Each element on the page should earn its place by answering one of the three. If an element answers none (bio paragraphs, brand story, outbound links), it is a candidate for removal.
- The lesson's two pro tips sit on top of this: show real personality ("a community, not a lecture hall") and name the specific transformation.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/16-task-2-about-page.md (the three-question framing and the two pro tips).

### Framework: About Page Anatomy (video + text + proof)
**When to use:** Deciding what physically goes on the page and in what order.
**Steps/Template:**
1. **Video, roughly 2 to 5 minutes**, built from the Promise-Pain-Proof script below.
2. **Text description under the video**, built from either the four-part template (Option 1) or the NOMAD generator (Option 2).
3. **Proof assets** next to or below it: screenshots of wins, testimonials, recent results.
4. **Visible price and urgency** inside the text block, not hidden.
- Things that belong: short punchy bullets, the specific list of what is inside (community, calls, classroom, member map), success stories and testimonials.
- Things that do not: filler slogans, the creator's biography, links out to other sites, big paragraphs.
**Example from source:** Nick Saraev's page layout (video, wall of wins images beside it, offer-first text, bonuses, scarcity line), described in transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [line ~355-455 of the transcript].

### Framework: About Video Script (Promise, Pain, Proof, Body, Objections, Close)
**When to use:** Recording the page video. The doc is a "Skool VSL Framework" meant to be copied as a structure, not read aloud verbatim.
**Steps/Template:**
| Section | Job | Template line |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Promise | Say what the viewer will get from watching; make staying worthwhile | "In this video I'll show you how to [specific outcome] without [common sacrifice]." |
| 1. Pain | Call out the frustrations they already feel | "If you've tried [failed attempt] and [frustrating situation]..." |
| 1. Proof | Name two or three before-and-after results | "I know this works because [Person] went from [before] to [after]..." |
| 2. Body | Connect each thing inside the group to the outcome | "[Item] so that you can [benefit]" (calls, community, training roadmap) |
| 3. Objections | Handle the top 3 to 5 doubts | What they think, why that is wrong, what is actually right, proof |
| 4. Close | Urgency plus a direct invitation | Price rising soon, limited spots, expiring bonus, early-member perk; then an explicit "see you inside" |
**Example from source:** linked-docs/task-2-about-page-1X4pEQC2.txt (weight-loss example used for the Promise/Pain/Proof lines).

### Framework: Text Template Option 1 (Four-Part Offer Page)
**When to use:** The creator has a concrete promise or guarantee and wants a compact, offer-first block under the video.
**Steps/Template:**
1. One line that states the community and highlights the outcome.
2. The guarantee or offer, if one exists.
3. What is inside, each item paired with why it matters to the member (value stacked, plus a separate bonuses block).
4. A call to action that creates urgency and restates the offer.
**Example from source:** linked-docs/task-2-about-page-130dIL2H.txt. Maker School's block opens with a "fastest and most affordable way to land client #1" line, states a first-client-in-90-days-or-refund guarantee, lists four core items "worth +$40K" and four bonuses "worth +$10K", then closes with a price-going-up line. The doc shows $204/mo while Task 1 uses $184/mo; the number moved as Nick raised prices, so the template matters more than the figure.

### Framework: NOMAD Generator (Template Option 2)
**When to use:** Drafting group description and about-page text quickly with any AI chat tool; the doc is a ready-to-paste prompt.
**Steps/Template:**
- **N**iche (who it serves), **O**utcome (the transformation), **M**echanism (the method or system), **A**uthority (proof, numbers, story), **D**eliverables (specific items inside, plus price and guarantee).
- The prompt makes the AI ask five questions one at a time and write nothing until all five are answered.
- Output: **3 group descriptions** (hard limit 150 characters each, aiming for 130 to 148) and **3 about-page versions** (hard limit 1,000 characters each, aiming for 900 to 985), each followed by its character count.
- Group description angles to vary: outcome-led, niche-led or power-claim ("THE #1..."), mechanism-led. One to three emojis, occasional caps on one phrase.
- About-page building blocks to mix: hook (direct address, problem agitation, or bold claim), up to three problem lines marked with an X emoji, intro/authority line, a "what's inside" header with checkmark lines, optional bonus lines, social proof numbers, price/guarantee, closing call to action. Bold-unicode sub-headers sparingly (maximum two). Voice is confident, warm, specific, never get-rich-quick.
**Example from source:** linked-docs/task-2-about-page-1mMiFuiS.txt. Its three samples show three shapes: an acting community that opens with a direct greeting and a "join by Sunday" deadline; a photo-organizing community that opens with the reader's overwhelm, adds three pain lines, then one credibility sentence; an AI-creator community that opens with rankings ("Top 1%", "#22 globally"), a spots-left-at-this-price line, and a lock-in-for-life price close.

### Framework: Setup Assistant Shortcut
**When to use:** Staring at a blank page. The Platinum assistant (a custom GPT) asks five questions and returns a first draft of the positioning-and-deliverables statement, the group description, and the about-page description. The lesson frames the result as a starting draft to tweak, not finished copy. Access needs a signed-in ChatGPT account.
**Steps/Template:** Answer the five questions with specifics, take the three drafts, then pressure-test them against the What/Who/Why test and the banned-phrase list below.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/14-platinum-setup-assistant.md and screenshot classroom/images/start-here/14-platinum-setup-assistant-1.png.

### Framework: Proof Stack (wins, testimonials, recency)
**When to use:** The page explains the offer but conversion is weak, or the creator feels they have "no wins yet".
**Steps/Template:**
1. Lead with the strongest proof format available: a video testimonial compilation, then long-form messages, then screenshots.
2. Favor recent results. Fresh outcomes read as "this still works".
3. Collect proof by asking: a direct message asking for a short video, with no incentive needed.
4. If there are no members yet, harvest wins from comments, DMs, and replies on public content and place them on the page.
5. Add a graphic of the classroom so people can see what they will unlock, and any special tools or access included.
6. Make the price feel small and the return feel large (a per-day comparison, an ROI frame).
**Example from source:** James Lu: a 26-minute student-testimonial video at the top of the page, followed by two walkthrough videos, a detailed student message, and result screenshots; he credits adding it with moving page conversion from 2.4% to 3.4% [transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md, line ~752-800]. Josh Madakor (about $140k MRR) uses a simple, lightly edited video of what is inside, as many wins as he can gather, a classroom graphic, and a per-day cost comparison [04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md, line ~528-557]. Nick Saraev loads the page with "hundreds of wins", reasoning that a buyer weighing an investment mostly wants to know whether others got a return [01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, line ~357-377].

### Framework: Boolean Promise and ROI Framing
**When to use:** The headline feels vague ("level up", "learn AI") or the offer needs a stronger reason to buy now.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Convert a fuzzy skill promise into a yes/no outcome the member can verify (get a client, hit a score, lift a weight).
2. If the exact outcome cannot be made binary, look for an adjacent outcome that covers roughly 80% of the need and can be.
3. Add a time box that feels believable (Nick tested 30 days, six months, and a year in his head before settling on 90 days).
4. Optional: back the promise with a refund guarantee. Nick estimates one to three percent of members ask for refunds, against a far larger lift in sign-ups.
5. Put the price and affordability near the top of the page, and frame the purchase as an investment with a quantified "what's inside is worth" line.
6. Repeat the same promise on the cover image, the bio, and the page so the offer reads identically everywhere.
**Example from source:** Nick Saraev, "land your first AI client in 90 days" [01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, line ~159-300]. He also worked the search term he expected people to type ("AI automation") into the page copy on purpose.

### Framework: Traffic-Before-Page Perspective
**When to use:** The creator keeps tweaking the page while traffic is thin, or is delaying launch for a perfect page or video.
**Steps/Template:**
- Think of the sequence as content sells the dream, a call to action points to the link, and the about page confirms. By the time visitors land, many are already persuaded.
- Jade (about $175k/mo) ran a page with roughly three sentences and a generic image and still passed $100k, because her Instagram content and chat automation had already sold the outcome. Her content shows lifestyle and results rather than listing modules.
- Samuel G (about $225k/mo, 10,000 members at $25) argues that if a page is changed from long to two sentences the conversion would hold, since people landing should already want in.
- A community thread cites an average paid-community page conversion near 4%, with some $100k+ communities converting around 1% on very high visitor counts (5,000+ in a week), so volume is the bigger lever.
- Oscar Patel moved his page video from long and scripted to short and authentic, and recommends changing one element at a time and leaving it a week before judging.
**Example from source:** 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [line ~154-230]; 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md [line ~291-307]; 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [line ~67-71, ~802-834]; community/questions/2026-10-02-traffic-traffic-traffic.md.

### Framework: The Cover Image 1-2 Second Test
**When to use:** Designing, auditing, or ranking cover image options.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Show the image to someone for one to two seconds, then take it away.
2. Ask what the community is about and why they would join.
3. If either answer is wrong or slow, the image fails, however good it looks.
- Stakes from the lesson: the cover is the creative Skool uses for ads and promotion. A weak one gets less ad spend allocated and fewer visits from the platform. Some communities grew dramatically on the strength of a single converting cover.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/17-task-3-cover-image-description.md.

### Framework: Cover Image Anatomy (three traits)
**When to use:** Building the brief for a designer, Canva, or an image generator.
**Steps/Template:**
| Trait | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Big bold benefit text | The headline states what joining gets you, in few words, readable at thumbnail size |
| Clear theme | The subject (tattooing, AI, health, trading) is recognizable without reading |
| Creator's face | A human face adds trust and a point of identification |
- The six reference covers in the lesson screenshot show the pattern: a faceless-YouTube-with-AI cover with a "join 2,600+ creators" badge beside the creator's portrait; a clean serif "health is looks" cover with a 7,300+ members count and before/after photos; an AI video-and-images cover with a cheerful creator and a pet; a "build an AI agent that manages your social media" cover with a price badge, a beginner-friendly badge, and a pointing creator; a tattoo-art cover reading "learn tattooing from beginner to pro"; and Maker School's "your first AI client in 90 days" with the creator and tool logos. Four of the six show the creator, one shows before/after faces of members, and one relies purely on theme artwork, so the face is a strong pattern rather than a hard rule. Numbers (members, price, level) appear on several.
- Field update 2026-10-09: Hormozi: a great cover photo WITH YOUR FACE, and promise an outcome, not a community. Staff review: the cover must make the niche outcome obvious (a uniform photo or "Get Military Ready" for a military prep group) and carry the value prop so Skool can run it as an ad [community/updates/2026-10-09-tips-to-get-more-traffic-from-skool-ads.md; community/learnings/2026-10-08-before-after-new-group-banner-photo.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/images/start-here/17-task-3-cover-image-description-1.png. The lesson also links a set of Canva cover templates.

### Framework: Growth Boost Cover Ad Path
**When to use:** Understanding why the cover image carries so much weight after launch.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Growth Boost is Skool's paid-acquisition program: Meta and Instagram ads sent to your group, plus placement in Skool search and the homepage.
2. It is on by default. Members it sends are charged a 30% share; members you bring organically pay only the standard transaction fee (around 2.9%).
3. The first ad every group runs is the cover image. It starts once the group has its first two or three members.
4. After the cover ad performs well, Skool may also promote your organic content, which tends to work better than the cover.
5. Profile socials (Instagram, X, YouTube) should be linked so Skool knows where to look.
6. Skool favors groups that send traffic, convert it, and keep members happy; building the group well is described as the surest way to get picked up, ahead of ad-optimizing tricks.
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/08-onboarding-call.md [line ~348-415, ~448-459]. Josh Madakor's team attributes a surge in network traffic to working through a Growth Boost checklist; the cover image was the hardest and last item they fixed [04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md, line ~373-385].

### Framework: Group Description Line
**When to use:** Writing the short text that sits with the cover image in discovery.
**Steps/Template:** One line, 150 characters maximum, led by niche-plus-outcome, a power claim, or the mechanism. Keep the same promise as the cover and page. Example shapes from the NOMAD doc: "Master [skill]. Then use it to [monetize/outcome]"; "THE #1 community for [specific people] to [3 outcomes]"; "[Audience]: no more [pain]! Just one simple system to [result]".
**Example from source:** linked-docs/task-2-about-page-1mMiFuiS.txt; classroom/start-here/17-task-3-cover-image-description.md (the task pairs cover image and description).

### Framework: Change-One-Thing Testing Loop
**When to use:** Optimizing a live about page, cover, or video without losing the signal.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Pick one element (video, headline, cover, bullets, price line).
2. Change only that and leave everything else alone for about a week (four days of falling sign-ups can still tempt an early pull, so decide the window in advance).
3. Compare sign-ups against the previous week with traffic roughly comparable.
4. Keep or revert, then pick the next element.
**Example from source:** Oscar Patel's approach, 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [line ~802-834]. Nick Saraev adds a practical wrinkle: renaming the group does not break existing share links, so names can be tested safely [01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md, line ~1246-1252].

## Key Principles
- The page exists to convince, so every block should answer what, who, or why; everything else tends to dilute it.
- A specific promise beats a broad one. "Land your first client in 90 days" is checkable; "become your best self" is not.
- Short beats long. Punchy bullets and one clear offer line are read; paragraphs are skipped.
- Authenticity outperforms production polish. Nick's half-hour talking-head video converted well, Oscar's shorter and more natural cut replaced a scripted one, and Josh keeps his lightly edited.
- Uploading the page video through Loom (rather than as a standard YouTube link) made the preview play moving frames and reportedly lifted conversion, at least when Nick tried it; the behavior may have changed since.
- Proof is the strongest asset on the page. Recent video testimonials and result screenshots do work that copy cannot.
- Name the concrete thing inside (calls, member map, templates) plus why it matters, rather than a vague "access".
- Authority is a line, not a chapter. NOMAD's samples fit one credibility sentence into the intro rather than a biography section.
- The mentors consistently report that pre-click persuasion (content, DMs, live streams, chat automation) drives conversion more than page edits.
- Cover images double as ads, so they need to work out of context in a feed, at small size, with no explanation.
- Using the same promise across cover, bio, group description, and page makes the offer feel coherent and searchable.
- Group description and about-page text have platform limits (150 and 1,000 characters in the NOMAD prompt); counting characters, emojis included, avoids having copy truncated.
- Optimize in place after launch; the page does not need to be perfect to start collecting evidence.

[Guarantee line: "Get [outcome] in [time] or [remedy]."]

What you get (worth [$_____]):
✅ [Item 1] - [why that matters to the member]
✅ [Item 2] - [why that matters to the member]
✅ [Item 3] - [why that matters to the member]
✅ [Live element: weekly call / coaching / feedback]

BONUSES (worth [$_____]):
🎁 [Bonus 1]
🎁 [Bonus 2]

[Proof line: "[___] members, [result], [ranking/award]"]
🚀 [Price]/mo [rising soon / founding rate for first ___ members]. Join now and [outcome promise].

### Banned-phrase and checklist audit
```
[ ] Opening line names who it is for and the outcome
[ ] No filler slogans (see Anti-Patterns)
[ ] No biography section; one authority line only
[ ] No links to outside sites
[ ] No paragraph longer than ~2-3 lines; bullets are short
[ ] What's inside is specific, each item tied to a benefit
[ ] Proof present: wins, testimonials, recent results
[ ] Price and urgency visible; guarantee stated if one exists
[ ] Video 2-5 minutes, conversational
[ ] Same promise appears on cover, bio, and description
```

## Decision Tools
- IF there is no video yet THEN publish the text block and proof first and add the video later, since the mentors launched with simple pages and improved over time (the community question on this topic had no captured answer, so this is inferred from the launch-first lessons).
- IF the offer headline is vague THEN rewrite it into a yes/no outcome plus a time box before touching design.
- IF the outcome cannot be made yes/no THEN look for an adjacent outcome covering about 80% of the value that can be checked.
- IF there is a guarantee THEN use Option 1 so the guarantee sits in line two.
- IF there is no guarantee and the creator wants speed THEN use NOMAD or the Setup Assistant to get three variants, then pick one.
- IF there are no wins yet THEN collect comments, DMs, and replies from content and place them on the page, and treat early members as the first testimonials.
- IF sign-ups are low and visitors are few THEN work on traffic and call to action first; page edits come second.
- IF sign-ups are low and visitors are plenty THEN review the video and proof first, and change one element per week.
- IF a visitor can name the theme but not the benefit in 1-2 seconds THEN rework the headline text of the cover.
- IF the creator is the brand THEN put the face on the cover, as four of the six reference covers do.
- IF the cover relies on a member before/after (health, fitness) THEN consider the transformation photo as the "face".
- IF the video is over about 5 minutes THEN trim it, or cut the part that covers things the text already says.
- IF the copy runs near the character cap THEN count again with emojis and line breaks included.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Banned filler phrases.** The lesson specifically rules out "No fluff, just results", "Remove the guesswork", "Stop overthinking", and "You don't need more ___, you need to know what actually works". They read as generic and could appear on any page; they also do not answer what, who, or why.
- **Bio on the page.** Credentials belong in one sentence of authority; a life-story section is a distraction at the moment of decision.
- **Outbound links.** Every external link is an exit from the conversion.
- **Walls of text.** Most visitors will not read four paragraphs, and Samuel G argues that reading more would not change the decision anyway.
- **Vague positioning.** "Level up", "unlock your potential", and "create a life you love" give no signal about audience or outcome.
- **Over-produced, slow, scripted video.** Both Oscar Patel and Nick Saraev moved away from slick scripted pitches toward a natural one; a mismatch between a casual personality and a scripted VSL creates a barrier.
- **Rewriting everything at once.** Changing video, page, structure, and call to action in the same week removes the baseline, so nothing can be learned.
- **Selling the course list on social content.** Jade skips "nine modules and daily support" in her videos and sells the outcome and lifestyle instead.
- **Cover images with tiny text or a clever-but-unclear concept.** If the benefit needs more than two seconds, it will lose to the next post in the feed.
- **Cover image as an afterthought.** It is the first ad Skool runs for the group; Josh Madakor's team left it for last because it was the hardest, and that is the one asset worth doing early.
- **Delaying launch for page perfection.** The mentors repeatedly state the page matters less than traffic and offer clarity.
- **A different promise on every surface.** Cover says one thing, page another, bio a third.
