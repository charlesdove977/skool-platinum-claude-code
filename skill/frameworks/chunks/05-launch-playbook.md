## Core Concept
A Skool launch is a traffic event, not a product event. The lessons build to one claim: make a clear offer, then point as much attention as possible at a single destination (the Skool about page) for a sustained stretch. Anticipation is manufactured before launch day with a short drip of teasers, and then every platform you own carries a prescribed minimum of posts for roughly two weeks, each one ending in a keyword or link that leads to the same place. Conversion mechanics (stories with a keyword, auto-DMs, video CTAs framed as a natural next step, a founding-member price that expires) matter, but the mentors in the interviews repeatedly say volume and consistency matter more than perfecting any one post.

## Frameworks

### Framework: 5-Day Tease Drip
**When to use:** The days just before launch, when you want eager buyers waiting on opening day instead of cold traffic. The lesson's logic chain is: something is coming, so anticipation builds, so buyers are eager on launch day, so signups and revenue rise.
**Steps/Template:**
| Day | Message purpose | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Announce that an announcement exists | Big news is coming, stay tuned, join the broadcast channel for live updates |
| 2 | Name the audience | "If you are [audience], I am building something for you" |
| 3 | Name the pain and the promise | You keep seeing people struggle with [pain]; you finally have something that helps them [outcome] |
| 4 | First deliverable peek | One thing you will get inside is [deliverable 1], which helps with [specific benefit]; that is a small part of it |
| 5 | Stack more deliverables, set the date | You will also get [deliverable 2] and [deliverable 3]; tomorrow the doors open and you see everything |
- Mechanics on Instagram: record an IG story of yourself speaking, CTA to join your Instagram broadcast channel (Instagram's own help page covers creating one), then drip more detail each day. The rule is to reveal in layers, not all at once.
- Mechanics on YouTube: same day-by-day script, posted as YouTube community posts. If a normal video is going out during the tease window, repeat the same messaging inside it.
- The program links a "Platinum Launch Assistant" custom GPT that generates the tease posts.
- Field update 2026-10-09: member test: humble framing ("I had this idea, it's not finished, here it is") beats "big news coming" [community/questions/2026-10-08-rate-my-5-day-launch-tease.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/23-teasing-the-launch.md. The lesson's screenshot is Alex Hormozi's own cryptic "save the date, expect fireworks" Skool post with a curiosity image, which drew 580 likes and 274 comments before any offer was shown (images/start-here/23-teasing-the-launch-1.png, via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md). A community member who had already posted five teaser stories asked how to vary launch stories without sounding repetitive (community/questions/2026-10-07-how-many-instagram-stories-should-i-make.md); no answer was captured.

### Framework: One-Destination Focus (the Magnifying Glass)
**When to use:** Any time a bio, CTA, or content plan starts pointing to more than one place.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Picture a magnifying glass held on one spot. Fire needs two things: concentration (all energy on one point) and time (holding it long enough).
2. Apply it to traffic. Ten links in a bio and five different CTAs scatter attention so everything gets slightly warm and nothing ignites.
3. Route every post, story, video, and CTA to one destination only: your Skool.
4. Hold that for a long stretch. The lesson's progression is spark, then flame, then fire.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/25-the-power-of-focus.md, whose graphic shows Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, and Gmail icons converging through "link in bio" onto "your Skool". Jade echoes it in her interview: everything her accounts do points back to one place, and she has posted her community every day, weekends included (transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md). Josh Madakor declines to sell merch on YouTube because a second link lowers clicks on the first (04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md).

### Framework: Traffic Arsenal and 15-Day Launch Timeline
**When to use:** Before launch week, to inventory every possible traffic source and then schedule the minimum posting cadence. The stated motivation is avoiding the regret of forgetting an asset such as a large email list.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Make your own copy of the Google Sheets workbook ("Launch Deliverables Timeline").
2. Delete rows for platforms you do not use; keep only what you own.
3. Read the pre-checked boxes as the recommended minimum. Add more checks if you can.
4. Each day, open the sheet, see what you committed to, and post it.
5. The lesson states that keeping the default schedule is recommended, because posting less may reduce results.
**The default cadence (15-day window, days numbered 1 to 15):**
| Tier | Source | Days checked | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Instagram Story | 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 | every other day |
| Primary | Instagram Reel | 1,4,7,10,13,15 | roughly every third day |
| Primary | YouTube Video CTA | 1,5,9,13 | every fourth day |
| Primary | YouTube Community Post | 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 | every other day |
| Primary | TikTok Post | 1,4,7,10,13,15 | roughly every third day |
| Primary | TikTok Story | 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 | every other day |
| Primary | Facebook Reel | 1,4,7,10,13,15 | roughly every third day |
| Primary | Facebook Story | 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 | every other day |
| Secondary (day 1 only) | X post, blast email list, free Skool community, LinkedIn, Reddit, WhatsApp group, Telegram channel, blog, newsletter, Discord, Threads, Snapchat | 1 | one announcement on launch day |
- Field update 2026-10-09: staff (Mark N.): secondary sources run on whatever timeline you like; daily on each is best for maximum traffic [community/questions/2026-10-08-traffic-arsenal-secondary-sources.md].
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/26-build-your-traffic-arsenal.md and linked-docs/build-your-traffic-arsenal-1alukftm.csv. Note that days 1, 3, 5... of Story and Community Post coincide, so on most odd days a creator is posting four story/community items across platforms, while reels and TikTok posts land on days 1, 4, 7.

### Framework: Instagram Launch Stack (Story, Reel, Link in Bio, DMs)
**When to use:** Converting Instagram followers into paying members during launch. The lesson names four levers and says all four are used together, with ManyChat (comment or keyword to auto-DM) added to lift conversion.
**Steps/Template:**
| Lever | Sequence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Story | Hook, Offer, CTA, Close | Hook stops the scroll and teases the reveal. Offer says what is happening in the group and why it helps. CTA asks viewers to comment/DM a keyword for access, which creates buying intent. Close is the automated DM with the pitch and the Skool link |
| Reel | Hook, Educate or Share Story, CTA, Close | Teach something or tell your transformation, give value, then the same keyword CTA and DM close |
| Link in bio | One link only | Replace all links with skool.com/yourgroup. Add a bio line that calls out the link (for example "Join my ___ community" with a pointing emoji). More links confuse and hurt conversion |
| DMs | Reply personally | Answer questions, start conversations, sell. Early one-to-one chats also surface objections, hesitations, and what people actually want, which you feed back into the offer |
- Hard rule from the lesson: do not post a story that is just the Skool link, because Instagram reduces reach on stories carrying links. Use the keyword route instead.
- The auto-DM is where the founding-member rate is mentioned, including the date it increases.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/27-instagram-launch-posts.md plus IMAGE-NOTES-B.md.
- A story from the "School of Mentors" account: hook frame with a credibility claim; offer frame promising weekly live Zoom calls with 8-10 figure entrepreneurs; CTA frame telling viewers to DM a keyword ("june"); automated close offering $29/mo instead of $49/mo for 24 hours with a "tell me more" button.
- Oscar Patel's version: hook is a pain statement ("I suffered"), story shows the problem and transformation, CTA frame shows his classroom grid, the auto-DM sells deliverables (routine videos, guides, accountability coach, 1:1 access, multiple Q&A calls weekly) and a button opens Skool, then a human-sounding follow-up asks whether the link worked.
- Oscar's bio shape: one-line promise, a DM-keyword line, "Join 7850+ members", and the Skool about link. Extra reel examples linked in the lesson: Steve Del Savio, Zack Ginn, Alan Powell.
- Oscar's interview adds that he launched mostly from TikTok with about 40,000 followers, made a story every day with a countdown, and mentioned the community at the end of every reel. He reports roughly 80 members at about $39/month on launch (transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md, launch answer; the transcripts carry no usable timestamps).

### Framework: YouTube Launch Stack (In-Video CTA, Pinned Comment, Descriptions, Channel Link)
**When to use:** Converting subscribers and the back catalog into members. Four levers: a 30-60 second CTA at the end of your next video, a pinned comment, an updated description on every video, and the channel link.
**Steps/Template:**
1. In-video CTA (30-60 seconds), covering four beats: your vision or reason for launching; what is inside and how it benefits the viewer; a literal look at the community (screenshots or walkthrough); urgency (for example, the first 25 members get the founding rate plus a 1:1 call).
2. Pinned comment: one line of outcome plus the Skool link, pinned on every video.
3. Descriptions: Skool link at the very top of each description, with the outcome promise as the first line.
4. Channel link: set the channel's link to skool.com/yourgroup.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/28-youtube-launch-posts.md. Nick Saraev's pinned comment is a single rocket-emoji line promising a first automation client in 90 days, with the Maker School link; his description opens with a fire-emoji line promising a guaranteed first customer and the about-page URL; his channel link is the Skool URL (images 28-youtube-launch-posts-1 to -3, via IMAGE-NOTES-B.md).

### Framework: Natural-Next-Step CTA Test
**When to use:** Deciding whether a YouTube end-CTA is framed well. The lesson's most-common-mistake warning is a random CTA bolted onto a video that never set it up.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Ask whether the community is the logical continuation of what the viewer just watched: "I enjoyed this free video and now want more."
2. Make sure the CTA states WHAT is in the community, HOW it helps, and WHO it is for.
3. Place it after real value, not instead of it.
**Example from source:** Two reference videos.
- Nolan Gouveia, an investing channel (transcripts/start-here/28-youtube-launch-posts-ref-4ItZFsE_gos.md). After roughly eight minutes of market analysis, the pivot comes at [0:07:59]: he tells viewers to watch a quick explainer, says he is moving his exclusive group from Patreon to Skool, and details the upgrade at [0:08:55] to [0:10:37]: weekly live call (previously twice a month), a members-only watchlist, monthly portfolio updates, exclusive videos, two ready courses, recorded calls, a calendar, and topic categories. He closes at [0:10:37] by pointing to the link below and the pinned comment, noting about 500 members already inside.
- Nick Saraev (transcripts/start-here/28-youtube-launch-posts-ref-jBF48jNWPJE.md). He spends the whole video walking through five automations a viewer could sell, then at [0:22:07] explains that templates for every system live in his community. He names the day-by-day roadmap and daily accountability, says it has 2,000+ members, and says the price rises every 100 members. The CTA reads as the place to get the blueprints for what was just shown.

### Framework: Bulk Description Edit
**When to use:** Day one of launch, to turn every old video into a traffic source.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Open YouTube Studio and use the bulk-edit descriptions tool (the lesson links Google's official instructions).
2. Add the Skool link and a one-line outcome at the top of every description.
3. One update covers the whole library.
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/29-bulk-edit-youtube-descriptions.md. Its screenshot is a Nick Saraev video with 1.9M views carrying the Maker School line first. Nick's interview describes his own soft launch the same way: late one night he added a "join my school community" line at the top of his YouTube links and woke up to a few hundred dollars of MRR (01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md). James Lu likewise says he mostly relies on the first link in the description rather than spoken CTAs (05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md).

### Framework: Five-Email Launch Sequence
**When to use:** Anyone with an email list. The lesson says email is highly effective, supplies two real examples, and a five-email template.
**Steps/Template:**
| # | Role | Subject idea | Body logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-launch | "I have been building something for [audience]" | Repeated question from the audience, what you built, open date, promise that list members see the founding offer first |
| 2 | Doors open | "[Community] is officially open" | Their real question in their words, why you built it, 3-4 specific deliverables, founding rate, deadline or member cap, a real capacity reason for the limit, P.S. with first call or event |
| 3 | First-week preview | "What your first week looks like" | Step by step: start-here task, first post you will review, weekly call; list of small wins by end of week; one clean classroom screenshot; founding deadline reminder |
| 4 | Objections | "A few questions about [community]" | Answer beginner fit, time required, difference from alternatives (no attacking), cancellation terms, first action; image of community; deadline |
| 5 | Final day | "Last call, founding access closes tonight" | Deliverables recap, price now versus after, exact deadline with time zone, a clear statement that this is the last launch email, a P.S. repeating deadline and link |
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/30-email-launch-copy.md contains two live examples. "Poop Scoop Academy" announces the wait is over, offers $29/month forever for 29 hours only (then $49), lists masterclasses, SOPs, vendor insights, and community, and adds a bonus that the first 20 sign-ups are entered to feature their dog in a board game; it also embeds a sneak-peek video. "Mikey No Code" offers the first 25 members the masterclass at $29/month (normally $299), notes course content goes live in a week, bulleted learning outcomes, and a P.S. that the early-bird deal happens once. The referenced video (transcripts/start-here/30-email-launch-copy-ref-7B5ZQuxuGm8.md, about 5:50) is the Poop Scoop sneak-peek: a walk through the first 15 of 32 classrooms (getting started, daily operations, authorized vendors, CRM tutorials, book club, bootstrap marketing, study-group calls, DISC assessment, masterclass recordings, business coaching), shot as a quick tour instead of a 20-60 minute video.
Interview corroboration: Samuel G's launch campaign was email-only. He emailed everyone who had attended his free Zooms announcing "our last Zoom"; about 150 showed up and about 70 joined the community that night (06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md). In the community feed, Member 2995 planned launch-day emails to his list, a video to socials, and a test of Skool's boost (community/wins/2026-10-07-accepted-today-launched-today.md).

### Framework: Founding-Rate Urgency Mechanics
**When to use:** Pricing the launch window so there is a real reason to join now.
**Steps/Template:** Pick one limiter and say it everywhere (auto-DM, email, YouTube CTA):
| Limiter | Source example |
|---|---|
| Time-boxed | $29 versus $49 for 24 hours (School of Mentors story DM); $29 forever for 29 hours (Poop Scoop email) |
| Count-boxed | First 25 members at founding price plus a 1:1 call (YouTube lesson); first 25 at $29 versus $299 (Mikey email); first 20 enter a bonus draw |
| Ladder | Price rises every 100 members (Nick Saraev, 28 transcript at [0:22:07]) |
| Announced increase | Josh Madakor announced price increases ahead of time; each one produced a rush of joins but also noisy churn and messy analytics (04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md) |
- The emails stress a believable reason for the limit (small calls, personal feedback, onboarding capacity).
- A community member asked how long to keep a $40-then-$50 early-bird open; the thread has no captured answer, so the sources offer only the 24-hour, 29-hour, and 25-member precedents above.

### Framework: Soft Launch and Launch-Before-Perfect
**When to use:** When you are tempted to keep polishing instead of exposing the offer to buyers.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Launch with something small (one module, a calendar of calls, a Start Here post).
2. Let early members tell you what to add; build in response.
3. Optionally soft-launch quietly, then escalate promotion.
**Example from source:**
- Nick Saraev: his first community was a simple course and a monthly call; he DMed 20 to 30 people on Twitter for feedback, collected the ways it fell short, and redesigned. He soft-launched at night in YouTube descriptions so he would not obsess over results. He favors a roughly 99 value to 1 pitch ratio and a first explicit pitch at the end of an hour-long video with a refund guarantee, reasoning that a monthly subscription rewards steady growth over a countdown-driven spike (01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md). He also reports YouTube drove roughly 20 times more conversions than Instagram despite similar follower counts.
- Jade ran with a single ebook and one module for about a year and a half; she moved 1,500 members from Instagram in an hour when she announced Skool (03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md).
- The onboarding call recommends launching now rather than spending weeks perfecting copy, and notes that the first-ad run on Growth Boost uses the cover image once the first one to three members join (08-onboarding-call.md).
- Community: a member's pinned post "Accepted yesterday, launched today" got 30 likes with the line "make it exist then make it better" (community/discussion/2026-10-06-accepted-yesterday-launched-today.md).

### Framework: Seven-Day Crunch Rule
**When to use:** Short on time before a deadline or ranking milestone.
**Steps/Template:** Oscar Patel's answer to "what if you only have seven days": raise content volume (or hold quality while increasing quantity) and raise CTA frequency, while announcing a dated event so there is something to count down to. Everything else is for a teammate or for a later date (02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md). The onboarding call adds that milestones start counting November 1, so launching earlier gives more runway (08-onboarding-call.md).

### Framework: Traffic Math
**When to use:** Judging whether a low conversion rate is the problem or the volume is.
**Steps/Template:**
- Lesson 24: the average $100k+/month community sees about 43,000 about-page views per month; the lesson says each Platinum member has 100k+ followers, so that scale is within reach.
- Community post by Member 3d1f: Sam Ovens put average about-page conversion for paid communities near 4%, while $100k+ MRR communities were screenshotted around 1% with 5,000+ about-page visitors in a week (community/questions/2026-10-02-traffic-traffic-traffic.md).
- Jade reports a 1.7% conversion rate on about 30,000 views and says she focuses on traffic, never on the percentage (03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md).
- A counter-case: Member 2875 Tripkovic paid $160 per $99 subscriber when running ads directly and asked whether to build a better funnel (community/questions/2026-10-07-drive-more-traffic.md; no answer captured).

## Key Principles
- Traffic is the single lever the Launch folder cares about; offer quality and traffic together decide the outcome.
- Anticipation is built, not hoped for: a five-day reveal ladder precedes the open.
- Reveal gradually. Each tease adds one new piece (audience, pain, deliverable, bundle).
- Every launch asset points at the same Skool about page; one bio link, one description link, one channel link.
- Keep the default posting cadence. The workbook is a floor, not a ceiling.
- On Instagram, use keyword-triggered DMs rather than story links, because story links lower reach.
- A CTA converts when it feels like the next step of the content, not an interruption.
- Show the actual community (screenshots, classroom) inside the CTA; people buy what they can picture.
- Put the outcome promise first in every pinned comment and description.
- Use urgency with a true reason (limited capacity, price increase on a date).
- DMs are market research: record the objections and fold them into the offer.
- Sequence emails as tease, open, preview, answer objections, final call.
- Old content keeps earning: update the entire back catalog once, with one edit.
- Mentors favor consistency over a spike: daily stories, evergreen videos, a link in every description.

## Templates and Fill-Ins

### 5-day tease drip (YouTube community posts and IG stories)
```
Day 1: Something big is coming for [audience]. Stay tuned. Join [broadcast channel / community tab] for live updates.
Day 2: If you are [target audience], I am building something for you.
Day 3: I keep seeing people stuck on [pain point]. I finally have something that helps you [valuable outcome].
Day 4: Sneak peek: inside you will get [deliverable #1], which helps you [specific benefit]. That is only a small piece.
Day 5: You also get [deliverable #2] and [deliverable #3], built to help you [outcome]. Tomorrow I show everything and open the doors.
Day 6: Doors open: [community name]. Founding rate [price] until [date/time or member cap]. Comment "[KEYWORD]" or join at [skool.com/_______________].
```

```
Frame 1 - HOOK: [Bold claim or pain line that makes them stay for frame 2]
```

## Decision Tools
- IF you have 1,000+ followers but an audience scattered across platforms, THEN choose the single platform where your content already earns the most and run the primary-tier cadence there first; Nick reports YouTube converting roughly 20 times better than Instagram for him.
- IF you cannot make a story without a link, THEN replace the link with a keyword CTA and deliver the link in the DM.
- IF you have an email list of any size, THEN include a blast email on day 1 and consider the full five-email sequence; Samuel G got about 70 joins from about 150 attendees on one invite.
- IF you have a YouTube library, THEN bulk-edit descriptions before posting a new video.
- IF your CTA feels bolted on, THEN ask whether the video's topic naturally leads to the community; if not, rewrite the topic or the bridge.
- IF you want urgency but have no real cap, THEN use a dated price increase and say it in the auto-DM.
- IF you have only a week, THEN apply the crunch rule: more content, more CTAs, and a dated launch event.
- IF your conversion rate looks low, THEN check traffic first; the sources show 1% to 1.7% converting acceptably at high volume.
- IF you are afraid the classroom looks empty, THEN note that Jade and Nick both started with minimal content and built based on member requests.
- IF you want to avoid launch-day anxiety, THEN consider Nick's approach of soft-launching at night with a description link.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Ten links in a bio or several competing CTAs:** attention scatters and nothing ignites.
- **Posting only the Skool link as an Instagram story:** the lesson says reach drops when stories carry links.
- **A random CTA on an unrelated video:** viewers do not see the community as a next step.
- **Revealing the whole offer on day one:** the drip loses its anticipation effect.
- **Posting less than the default cadence:** the lesson warns that results may fall.
- **Ignoring DMs after stories and reels:** you lose sales plus free market research on objections.
- **Forgetting owned channels such as an email list:** the traffic arsenal exercise exists to prevent it.
- **Waiting weeks to polish before launching:** members can request what they need after joining, and Platinum's rankings reward earlier starts.
- **Price-rise stunts without a plan:** Josh Madakor saw joins spike, but with noisy churn and analytics.
- **Heavy countdown selling for a long-term subscription:** Nick argues a gentle pitch ratio builds steadier growth, though he acknowledges a hard launch would lift short-term revenue.
