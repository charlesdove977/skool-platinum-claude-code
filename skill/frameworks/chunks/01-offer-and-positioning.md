## Core Concept
In Platinum, an offer is a promise about who changes and how, said so plainly that the right stranger recognises themselves in one sentence. The about page, cover, deliverables and price all hang off that sentence, which is why the classroom starts here and budgets only 2-4 hours for the whole setup. The $100k+ owners made the promise observable (a yes/no result, a number, a deadline), attached it to something people already spend money on, backed it with other people's wins, and treated the community and classroom as the mechanism rather than the product. When an offer stalls, the program's own diagnosis is short: the outcome is not specific enough, or people do not want what is being sold.

## Frameworks

### Framework: The Setup Triad (Positioning + Deliverables + Price)
**When to use:** Day one, before any page, post or video. This is Step 1 of the six setup steps (the others: about page, cover image and description, Start Here post, classroom plan, kickoff call).
**Steps/Template:**
| Piece | Question it answers | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning statement | Why would people join? | One sentence: who, what changes, by what method |
| Deliverables | What do they get inside? | 3 defaults + 2-4 custom lines, each tied to a benefit |
| Price | How is it priced? | A monthly number (plus annual) chosen from a niche band |
- Write answers in a blank Google Doc first, then compress. The program also ships a free ChatGPT "Platinum Setup Assistant" that asks 5 questions and drafts positioning and deliverables, a group description and about-page copy; it is positioned as a rough first draft to be edited, not a finished asset.
- Whole setup block is framed as launch-ready in roughly 2-4 hours; Hormozi's onboarding video says members who launch fastest earn the most, and members who have not launched within the first week or two risk removal.
**Example from source:** Maker School (Nick Saraev) shown as the worked triad: positioning "fastest and most affordable way for AI freelancers to get their first client for AI services through a day-by-day accountability system"; six deliverable lines; $184/month or $1,104/year. classroom/start-here/15-task-1-positioning-deliverables-pricing.md; classroom/start-here/13-the-6-setup-steps.md; classroom/start-here/14-platinum-setup-assistant.md; transcripts/start-here/01-1-hormozi-onboarding-video.md [0:02:27].

### Framework: Positioning Statement Formula
**When to use:** Writing or auditing the one-line description of the group; also when a cover, bio or CTA feels vague.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Answer four questions in plain language: (a) Who exactly is the audience? (b) What outcome do you deliver and how does their life change? (c) Generally how do you get them there? (d) Why should they learn it from you, what is your proof?
2. Fill the sentence: `[Skool name] helps [specific people] to [specific outcome] by [method]`.
3. Strip every word that could appear in a competitor's version of the sentence.
| Group | Who | Outcome | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maker School | AI freelancers | first client for AI services | day-by-day accountability system |
| Project Bellplex | men | functional strength, conditioning, mobility | at-home kettlebell program |
| Blue Collar Biz Accelerator | home service business owners | never run out of leads, hire right, scale | 1:1 implementation of a proven sales, marketing, hiring system |
| The Lady Change | women aged 40-75 | lose weight, get healthier, regain confidence | direct coaching, joint-friendly workouts, hormone-friendly meal plans |
- The lesson's own fail examples ("level up in life through mindset, discipline, personal growth" and similar) share one flaw: they fit almost any audience and name no observable change.
- Variants seen in the course: the offer-creation prompt uses `I help [specific person] achieve [specific result] by [timeframe or method]`; a member's draft used the same shape with a timeframe and a second promise ("lean and defined in 90 days, bounce back from festivals in half the time").
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/15-task-1-positioning-deliverables-pricing.md (formula, four questions, four examples, three failing examples); community/discussion/2026-10-07-help-with-positioning-statement.md (a member's draft, no captured feedback).

### Framework: Specificity Test (synthesised from the lesson's rules)
**When to use:** After drafting a positioning line, offer statement or cover headline.
**Steps/Template:**
| Check | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Audience swap | Swapping in a different audience makes the sentence wrong |
| Observable result | Someone else could watch and say "yes, that happened" |
| Named method | The "by ___" slot says how (system, coaching, program), not just "guidance" |
| Zero filler | Removing any word loses information |
| One-breath read | A stranger gets who/what/how in a single read |
- The prompt's rule of thumb for weak results: vague outcomes like "feel better", "grow my business", "gain confidence" get rewritten as countable results ("sign the first three clients", "publish 30 videos", "perform a full 60-second routine").
**Example from source:** classroom/start-here/15-task-1-...md (zero-filler principle); linked-docs/offer-not-converting-1096vhdr.txt Step 4 (vague-to-specific list).

### Framework: Deliverable Lines
**When to use:** Turning "what's inside" into something a visitor reads as value rather than a feature dump.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Start with the three items every Skool already has: a live weekly Q&A (ask anything, get direct feedback), the member map (find people near you), and the community forum (build alongside other founders).
2. Add 2-4 custom lines in the form `[what's inside] - [why that matters to them]`.
3. Put the strongest line first and give numbers where true.
**Example from source:** Maker School's list: $21K in software discounts to tools members already use, 40+ copy-paste templates/scripts for content, lead gen and sales, 3 lead-gen methods for high-ticket clients, live coaching, member map, community. Nick explains the page logic: offer as high on the screen as possible, lean on affordability, and show ROI ("what's inside worth $40,000", a $100 outlay set against a possible $50,000 return); he admits the $40,000 is an informal estimate. transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [~0:17:40-0:20:30]. A related contrast from Jade: on social she sells the lifestyle and result, not the module count; the detail lives in her DM automation. transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [~0:09:30].

### Framework: Boolean (Binary) Outcome and Risk Reversal
**When to use:** The promise sounds fine but cannot be measured, or conversion is low even with traffic.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Ask: if I sold this by cold call, what yes/no result could I promise? Nick's test question: did the customer get it or not?
2. Convert a skill promise ("learn AI") into a result a third party verifies ("get your first paying client for an AI service").
3. Choose the deadline by testing your own belief: he judged two weeks too short, a year achievable, then tried six months and settled on 90 days.
4. Add risk reversal if you can stand behind it (90 days or money back).
5. If the result cannot be made binary, look for an adjacent result that covers about 80% of the original value and can be.
- Economics he gives: refund requests ran about 1-3% of net revenue versus a signup lift he puts around 3x (self-reported, not audited); about 10 refund requests in a recent month against far more joiners. Members who finish the 90 days without a client but want to stay get a month refunded as goodwill, which he says produces louder wins later.
- James Lu's hard-mode variant: students, seasonal demand, not a money niche. His group title names the number (the 1550 formula), the real pitch is getting people through plateaus faster than studying alone, and any member who says the score did not improve is refunded (he has refunded all six months paid).
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [~0:07:30-0:12:30]; transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [~0:03:40-0:06:30, ~0:35:00]; transcripts/start-here/02-2-100k-month-examples.md [0:17:34-0:20:12].

### Framework: Outcome Ladder and the B2B Reframe
**When to use:** The niche feels crowded or the group reads as "education about a topic".
**Steps/Template:**
1. Write the ladder from topic to result, one rung per line.
2. Move up until the top rung is a result a buyer can value in money or time.
3. Pitch the top rung; the topic becomes the vehicle.
| Rung | Maker School version | Knitting version (Nick's illustration) |
|---|---|---|
| Learn the topic | learn no-code and AI | learn about knitting |
| Make money with it | learn to make money with AI | learn to make money with knitting |
| Specific, timed result | first paying AI customer in 90 days or money back | make $1,000/month from a knitting business in about five hours a week |
- Why it works in his telling: business-minded buyers think in ROI, so a price ($15/month in his knitting example) is easy to justify against a large return. He describes converting an AI-education (consumer-style) market into an AI-for-money (business-style) market, and calls the result "AI adjacent" so the group rides AI's excitement without being only about AI.
- The warning inside the advice: niche down to a problem people pay to solve, not to something esoteric (his joke example was medieval history from 1459 to 1512).
- Counter-example worth holding: a colour-grading community (Cullen Kelly) had 300+ members at $200/month with 100K+ followers, and a host noted it is not very "transformational" yet works, and Oscar's advice to a designer was to build around the skill you love and match it to a real need. The ladder is a strong default, not the only route.
**Example from source:** transcripts/start-here/02-2-100k-month-examples.md [0:17:34-0:20:59]; transcripts/start-here/01-1-hormozi-onboarding-video.md [0:04:09]; transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [~0:44:00-0:48:00].

### Framework: Six-Step Offer Creation Flow (the Skool Offer Creation Prompt)
**When to use:** Building an offer from scratch, repairing one that "is not converting", or gap-checking an offer you already like. The Need Help page sends members here first.
**Steps/Template:** The prompt turns an AI into an interactive offer strategist. It asks 1-3 questions at a time, prefers multiple choice, challenges vague answers, shows "Step X of 6" in each reply, refuses to call the group a "course", and deliberately avoids modules, bonuses, guarantees and pricing.
| Step | Goal | What gets pulled out | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Accomplishments | Find your credible proof | 2-3 things you achieved that others struggle with (lost weight, built a business, landed a first client) | Most valuable, teachable accomplishment chosen |
| 2 Transformations | Generate options | 3-5 "I help [person] go from [painful before] to [after] by [method]" lines, each with who, result, why you qualify, opportunity strength | You pick the 1-2 most exciting, believable, valuable, realistic |
| 3 Best-fit member | Define the person | Situation, what they tried, current struggle, experience level, what they want but lack; asks for one real person as an example | Three polished member descriptions, one chosen |
| 4 Exact result | Make it observable | What they can do or have by the end, how they know it worked, a realistic timeframe | Three increasingly specific result versions |
| 5 Pressure test | Would they pay? | Where the pain shows up, cost of staying stuck 6-12 months, prior attempts, existing spend, a 7-14 day quick win, why you versus free content | Rating table below, honest improvements |
| 6 Finalise | Lock and validate | Three versions: simple, emotional, specific | Offer Snapshot plus three validation moves |
- Step 5 ratings (Low/Medium/High unless noted): pain level; urgency (Someday/Soon/Right now); result clarity; ability to pay; credibility; differentiation. Fixes offered for weak scores: narrow the audience, raise urgency, sharpen the result, pick a stronger outcome, adjust the timeframe, clarify why your approach differs, choose a transformation your experience supports.
- Offer Snapshot contents: who it is for, the problem, the desired result, timeframe or method, why your approach is different, the final offer statement, one realistic 7-14 day quick win.
- Validation moves: interview five matching people, share the statement and ask what is unclear or unappealing, invite a small founding group to test the transformation.
- Field evidence: one member pasted the prompt plus his existing offer into his own Claude project and asked what was missing, and said it surfaced gaps in an offer he thought was solid; another member's screenshot credits the prompt for a rewritten about page that led to recording a VSL.
**Example from source:** linked-docs/offer-not-converting-1096vhdr.txt; offer-creation-prompt.md; classroom/need-help/02-offer-not-converting.md; community/discussion/2026-10-05-skool-offer-creation-prompt.md.

### Framework: Niche and Differentiator Moves
**When to use:** The topic is saturated, you doubt your edge, or you are choosing between audiences.
**Steps/Template:** Moves the mentors actually made:
| Move | Who / how | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Solve your own problem first | Oscar fixed his own sleep, teeth and posture issues over years, then built "health equals looks" (tagline: improve people's looks by fixing their health) against the usual cosmetic looksmaxing angle | $39/month, ~7.9k members, 80 joiners on launch day from a ~40k TikTok following |
| Own a planet, do not borrow one | Oscar: niche so narrowly that other topics orbit you; bring every subject back into your niche instead of expanding to others' niches | He talks only about the face and links body topics to it |
| Teach to the least informed listener | Samuel breaks real-estate creative financing down for people with no background | 10K members at $25/month |
| Authentic learner voice | Jade posted her learning process, wins and losses rather than posing as a guru | ~$155K MRR, 88% engagement, 77% retention (her figures) |
| Reputation first | Josh's offer converts because years of accurate content made people assume the paid product is good; he moved from 1:1 calls to a course to a community while the core outcome (jobs in IT/cyber) stayed constant | ~$130-140K MRR from under 2,000 members |
| Hard mode on purpose | James teaches SAT to teens who have little money, in a seasonal market, and still passed $100k/month | $99/month |
| Avoid "does everything" topics | Oscar would not launch a "life maxing" community; a vague aspiration nobody can be the best at | Matches the "don't niche up" advice |
**Example from source:** transcripts/100k-mo-interviews/02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [~0:03:30-0:11:30, ~0:30:00]; 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [~0:03:00, ~0:06:00]; 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md [~0:06:44-0:10:30]; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [~0:00:00]; 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md [~0:12:00].

### Framework: Access-Over-Content Value Proposition
**When to use:** You fear that giving away free content leaves nothing to sell, or members ask "why pay when YouTube is free?"
**Steps/Template:**
1. List what you give away free (concepts, frameworks, demos).
2. List what free content cannot do: answer a person's question, review their work, hold them to a schedule, troubleshoot their edge cases, be there daily.
3. Write the offer around the second list.
- Member f1e9 Bolt launched a pay group with a 1-minute-20-second iPhone video about a vision (improve 1% a week), no course, no PDFs, no VSL, no free trial: about 5% conversion, 200 members in a week, 255 later. The hosts' read: "the thing inside was him", and members value live coffee-hour-style access over libraries.
- Oscar sells time saved (a routine in 10-15 minutes, accountability) after giving away the framework publicly; Jade removed the free lives and replies to DMs she used to give on Instagram and offers them only inside; Nick answers every post himself and says that presence is part of what members pay for; James splits it as free YouTube = learn the concepts, paid = plateau troubleshooting, a schedule, software and edge cases; Samuel's offer is hand-holding through phone calls and paperwork.
- Jade's framing: creators who win treat the community as the asset and the social account as promotion for it.
**Example from source:** transcripts/start-here/11-it-s-really-that-simple.md [0:00:31-0:01:20]; 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [~0:16:50]; 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [~0:30:56]; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [~0:13:30]; 06-samuel-g-q-a-225k-mo.md [~0:21:55].

### Framework: Proof Stack
**When to use:** No customers yet, or customers exist but the page does not show it.
**Steps/Template:** Ordered from "available today" to "built over time":
1. Your own transformation or credential (Step 1 of the offer prompt). Oscar repeats his before/after in reels and posts a transformation story daily; the hosts note two weeks of stories equals ~14 pieces of proof.
2. Screenshots of existing messages and comments praising your content (James did this before any paid customer, pinned in his free group).
3. Wins already sitting in your comment section or DMs (Josh).
4. Video testimonials: DM past students, ask for a phone video, make it effortless ("I can edit the bad parts"); about half agree; incentives did not seem to matter. James's about page opens with a ~26-minute compilation and his conversion rose from 2.4% to 3.4% after adding it; recent results (latest test cycle) land harder than old ones.
5. A growing wins board: Nick's page carries many win screenshots next to the video and a sidebar "best of" list; the logic is that a buyer's first question is whether others got a return.
**Example from source:** 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [~0:28:40-0:33:00, ~0:40:00]; 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [~0:13:40, ~0:19:00]; 04-josh-madakor-q-a-130k-mo.md [~0:43:00]; transcripts/start-here/02-2-100k-month-examples.md [0:45:14-0:46:00].

### Framework: Market-Test the Offer Before Building It
**When to use:** You are tempted to spend weeks on a curriculum before anyone has bought.
**Steps/Template:**
1. Build the smallest version (Nick: a 14-day agency program, only the first day or two recorded at launch).
2. Invite warm people by DM with an honest, low-pressure ask; Nick sent a customised template to about 20 contacts and had ~20 members on day one, seeded with members who had real results.
3. Ask them what is wrong; he collected a list of why the first version "sucked" and rebuilt it.
4. Soft-launch in a low-stakes way (he posted links in YouTube descriptions late at night and woke to a few hundred dollars of MRR).
5. Let member requests drive additions (Jade went from one ebook to nine modules this way; James's first big product started as an internal flow chart for his team).
- Oscar's version: sell to yourself first; a member Priestley's line Nick repeats is "test in the market not your mind".
**Example from source:** 01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [~0:02:30-0:04:10]; transcripts/start-here/12-launch-first-build-later.md [0:00:00]; 03-jade-q-a-175k-mo.md [~0:03:00]; 05-james-lu-q-a-100k-mo.md [~0:18:30].

### Framework: One Promise Everywhere
**When to use:** The promise is decent but different surfaces say different things.
**Steps/Template:** Repeat the same promise on cover, bio, about headline, group description and CTA. Nick used his 90-day first-client promise in all of them and noted the community name can be changed without breaking links, because the URL is separate from the name, so names can be tested freely. Oscar closes nearly every video with the same phrase. The Big Lesson adds the commitment side: top creators made Skool their single offer and only bio link, though this is described as a best practice, not a rule.
**Example from source:** 01-nick-saraev-q-a-250k-mo.md [~0:07:00, ~0:58:00]; 02-oscar-patel-q-a-6-figure-mo.md [~0:22:41]; classroom/start-here/09-9-the-big-lesson.md.

### Framework: Naming and Repositioning Questions (open questions, not settled rules)
**When to use:** Choosing a group name or shifting audience on an existing membership.
**Steps/Template:** Two member posts show the decision shape; neither has captured answers, so treat them as prompts.
- Name vs promise: one member weighed an attitude-heavy name that overlaps with productivity groups against a plain "AI Marketing by [name]" that states the job and builds a personal brand; his promise line was "your next client, found by AI" via a 30-day sprint to 3 qualified calls. Test: put each name beside the promise as it would appear in Discovery.
- Repositioning: a Kajabi membership (marketed to 100,000+ people as chronic-health root-cause help) pivoting to longevity/healthy aging with ~70% of content unchanged. The question is whether the old name carries equity or old associations.
**Example from source:** community/questions/2026-10-07-launching-tomorrow-stuck-on-the-name-which-would-you-click.md; community/discussion/2026-10-06-membership-repositioning-rebranding-question.md.

## Key Principles
- Decide the offer from the buyer's side: who they are, what they want, what proves it worked. Nick tested a bare-bones version with real people and rebuilt from their criticism; Oscar began by selling to the person he used to be.
- Make the result countable. "Get a client in 90 days", "1550+", "buy a house without a bank" can all be checked; "level up" cannot.
- Use the deadline as part of the promise. Nick tested 14 days, 1 year, 6 months and 3 months against his own belief before committing.
- Treat guarantees as math: a small refund rate against a large conversion lift is a net gain, but only if you can really deliver.
- Aim for a niche where winning is described in dollars, hours or a number, yet remember colour grading, looks and guitar-style hobby niches have worked too.
- The classroom, calls and content are the mechanism; sell the outcome. The hosts in Josh's call put it this way: coaching, course and community changed shape for him while the core outcome stayed the same, which is why people kept buying.
- Free content can be generous; the paid group sells what free content cannot (answers, review, accountability, hand-holding, software, presence).
- Collect proof from day zero, even screenshots of comments, and ask for video testimonials with minimal friction.
- A bland-looking about page can still work if the pre-click content does the selling (Jade's page had three sentences and a stock photo); the offer line must still be specific.
- Keep the group focused on one offer; Oscar says he turned down brand deals and funnel pitches to stay on one thing.
- Do not wait for a perfect curriculum; Josh, Jade and James all describe adding to the classroom after members arrived.
- If a segment of your audience cannot afford the price, the offer may need a smaller, cheaper entry point rather than more persuasion (see Nick's regret about his price).

## Decision Tools
- IF the positioning sentence could be spoken by any coach in your field THEN narrow the audience or add a countable outcome and a named method.
- IF the result cannot be answered yes/no THEN look for an adjacent result that captures ~80% of the value and can.
- IF traffic and views are decent but few buy (one member had ~5,363 live viewers, 142 new followers and 2 sales) THEN suspect the offer line and CTA before adding reach; run the Pressure Test (Step 5) ratings on pain, urgency and clarity.
- IF you have never sold anything and your topic is a hobby THEN try the Outcome Ladder to a money or time result, or accept that a love-of-the-skill offer (colour grading, guitar) can still sell if the transformation is visible.
- IF you cannot make a guarantee without hurting yourself THEN use a softer risk-reversal (refund on request when a member is unhappy, as James and Samuel do) and watch refund rates.
- IF members ask "why pay when your videos are free?" THEN point to what free cannot do and say plainly that they can try the free material first.
- IF you have an existing offer you like THEN run it through the Offer Creation Prompt as a gap check rather than starting over.
- IF you are changing audience on an existing brand THEN decide whether the old name's associations hurt or help before investing in new assets.
- IF you have no proof yet THEN screenshot every kind comment/DM, post your own transformation daily, and start collecting video testimonials with your first members.
- IF the niche has no obvious dollar outcome THEN use a time, skill or lifestyle result you can show visually (Oscar's before/after is the model).

## Anti-Patterns
- "Mindset, discipline and personal growth" style positioning: no audience, no outcome, no method, so nobody recognises themselves.
- Selling a skill ("learn AI") instead of a result ("get a paying client"): the result is harder to dispute and easier to price.
- Spending weeks on a perfect classroom and hours editing a video for a group with no traffic (a member doing this is mentioned in the Member f1e9 Bolt clip).
- Hiding behind a guarantee you cannot deliver, or offering one with no plan for refunds.
- Describing features ("workouts, calls, community") to cold social audiences instead of the result; Jade's feed sells lifestyle and results, with detail reserved for the DM or page.
- Spreading across several offers or bio links; the Big Lesson and Oscar's refusals point the other way (a best practice, not a rule).
- Copying a mentor's tactic without their context; Josh says it is hard to advise because the hidden variables (reputation, face, audience, niche) differ for everyone.
- Starting with an offer tailored to what you want to teach rather than what the right person wants to buy; the Offer Prompt's Step 5 exists to catch this.
- Expecting a polished about page to rescue an unclear offer; Jade and Samuel both say the pre-click content does the selling.
- Claiming made-up proof; the prompt itself forbids inventing experience or results.
